\section{Optimal Transport Identification of ADM Mass}
\label{app:TransportMass}

This appendix records a speculative optimal-transport route to ADM mass. It is included as a research note parallel to Program D in the overview, not as a rigorous alternative proof used by the manuscript.

\subsection{Formal Transport Setup}
Let $(M, g)$ be a complete AF Riemannian manifold. The Wasserstein-2 distance between probability measures $\mu_0, \mu_1$ on $M$ is:
\begin{equation}
    W_2(\mu_0, \mu_1)^2 := \inf_{\gamma \in \Pi(\mu_0, \mu_1)} \int_{M \times M} d_g(x, y)^2 \, d\gamma(x, y),
\end{equation}
where $\Pi(\mu_0, \mu_1)$ is the set of couplings.

\begin{definition}[Asymptotic Cost Function]
For $x \in M$ and a ``point at infinity'' $\infty$, define the asymptotic squared distance:
\begin{equation}
    c_\infty(x) := \lim_{y \to \infty} \left( d_g(x, y)^2 - d_g(o, y)^2 \right),
\end{equation}
where $o$ is a fixed basepoint \emph{provided this renormalized limit exists}. Establishing such a limit and extracting its first-order asymptotics are part of the open transport program, not proved in this appendix.
\end{definition}

\begin{theorem}[Conjectural ADM Mass via Optimal Transport]\label{thm:ADMTransport}
If the renormalized transport-to-infinity functional can be defined with sufficient asymptotic control, then one expects it to determine the ADM mass of an asymptotically flat end.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Heuristic Derivation]
The expected derivation has the following ingredients:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Renormalized cost at infinity:} choose points $x_R$ escaping along the asymptotic end and study $d_g(x,x_R)^2 - d_\delta(x,x_R)^2$ after subtracting the Euclidean leading term.
    \item \textbf{Kantorovich duality:} reinterpret the renormalized cost through dual transport potentials.
    \item \textbf{Mass extraction:} identify the first non-Euclidean term in that expansion with the ADM coefficient of the asymptotically flat metric.
    \item \textbf{Comparison with energy/capacity:} relate the resulting transport quantity to the variational energies that appear elsewhere in the manuscript.
\end{enumerate}

None of those steps is completed here with the precision needed for a theorem. In particular, this appendix does not establish a rigorous renormalized transport formula for $M_{\ADM}$, and it does not use such a formula anywhere in the proof of the spacetime Penrose inequality.
\end{proof}

\subsection{Possible Relation to Penrose}
If a transport characterization of ADM mass were proved, one could try to combine it with lower bounds for transport cost in terms of horizon geometry. The present manuscript does \emph{not} establish those lower bounds, nor does it derive the Penrose inequality by this route. Accordingly no alternative proof claim is made in this appendix.
