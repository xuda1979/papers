\section{Conclusion and Outlook}
\label{sec:FinalConclusion}

We have analyzed the Spacetime Penrose Inequality for asymptotically flat spacetimes satisfying the Dominant Energy Condition. Penrose's 1973 conjecture predicts that for any trapped surface $\Sigma$:
\begin{equation*}
    M_{\mathrm{ADM}}(g) \ge \sqrt{\frac{A(\Sigma)}{16\pi}},
\end{equation*}
with equality if and only if the data embed isometrically into the Schwarzschild spacetime.

\subsection{Status of the Spacetime Penrose Inequality}

The original 1973 conjecture of Penrose \emph{assumed} weak cosmic censorship. In Section~\ref{sec:ProgramF} we record the corresponding Lorentzian route: if one supplements weak cosmic censorship by a global area comparison between trapped surfaces and event-horizon cross-sections, then the inequality $M_{\mathrm{ADM}} \geq \sqrt{A(\Sigma)/(16\pi)}$ follows from Hawking's area theorem, the Kerr bound, and Bondi mass loss.

The following results have been established with complete rigor:
\begin{itemize}
    \item For the outermost MOTS $\Sigma^*$, the inequality holds \textbf{provided the favorable jump condition $\tr_{\Sigma^*} k \ge 0$ is satisfied} (Theorem~\ref{thm:penroseinitial}). This condition is not automatic from stability alone and remains an explicit hypothesis for $k \neq 0$.
    \item In the time-symmetric case ($k=0$), the inequality holds for any trapped surface.
    \item In the direct MOTS regime (the surface under consideration is a MOTS and satisfies $\tr_\Sigma k \geq 0$ pointwise), the inequality holds.
\end{itemize}

The following cases remain conditional or open:
\begin{itemize}
    \item The compactness program for general trapped surfaces remains conditional. In particular, the route from a comparison surface to the pointwise favorable jump needed by the direct Jang theorem is not completed for $k \neq 0$.
    \item For general trapped surfaces without cosmic censorship, the present initial-data reduction is incomplete. In particular, the naive area comparison with the apparent horizon fails in binary merger counterexamples.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Summary of Established Results}

\textbf{Classical Approach (Sections~\ref{sec:Jang}--\ref{sec:Consolidated}):}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Jang Reduction:} The generalized Jang equation transforms the spacetime data $(M,g,k)$ into a Riemannian manifold $(\bar{M}, \bar{g})$ with cylindrical ends over the horizons.
    \item \textbf{Conformal Sealing:} A conformal factor $\phi \le 1$ seals the cylindrical ends while preserving nonnegative scalar curvature.
    \item \textbf{Smoothing:} Corner singularities are smoothed while maintaining $R \ge 0$.
    \item \textbf{AMO Monotonicity:} The $p$-harmonic level set method establishes the inequality on smooth approximants.
    \item \textbf{Result:} A rigorous direct proof for MOTS with favorable jump $\tr_\Sigma k \ge 0$, together with conditional reduction programs for more general trapped surfaces under compactness or Lorentzian area-comparison hypotheses.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Fundamental Obstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:Obstruction}):}
We prove that conformal methods \textbf{cannot} simultaneously achieve area preservation and mass reduction for the unfavorable case $\tr_\Sigma k < 0$. This explains why all classical approaches require additional assumptions.

\textbf{Lorentzian Approaches:}
Lorentzian optimal transport and causal methods offer promising alternative approaches, though significant mathematical challenges remain. These include:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Adapting Sturm--Lott--Villani theory to Lorentzian geometry.
    \item Establishing monotonicity of Geroch-type functionals under NEC.
    \item Connecting asymptotic quantities to ADM mass.
\end{enumerate}
Such approaches may provide conditional results equivalent to cosmic censorship assumptions.

\subsection{Why Causal Methods Are Promising}

The fundamental obstruction (Theorem~\ref{thm:Obstruction}) shows that conformal methods cannot simultaneously achieve area preservation and mass reduction for the unfavorable case $\tr_\Sigma k < 0$. Causal/spacetime approaches may bypass this obstruction because:

\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{No metric change:} Working directly in spacetime avoids conformal transformations.
    \item \textbf{Causal structure:} The 4-dimensional causal geometry encodes information lost in 3-dimensional reductions.
    \item \textbf{Natural monotonicity:} Hawking's area theorem and its generalizations suggest monotone quantities exist.
    \item \textbf{Global structure:} Optimal transport naturally connects local geometry to asymptotic quantities.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{However}, significant mathematical challenges remain for the fully unconditional case.

\subsection{Historical Context}

The Penrose inequality is a cornerstone of the cosmic censorship program and black hole thermodynamics. This work builds on decades of contributions:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Positive Mass Theorem:} Schoen--Yau (1979), Witten (1981)
    \item \textbf{Riemannian Penrose Inequality:} Huisken--Ilmanen (2001), Bray (2001)
    \item \textbf{Jang Equation Approach:} Schoen--Yau (1981), Bray--Khuri (2010)
    \item \textbf{MOTS Theory:} Andersson--Metzger (2009), Eichmair (2013)
    \item \textbf{Lorentzian Optimal Transport:} McCann (2020), Mondino--Suhr (2023)
\end{itemize}

Lorentzian optimal transport is a promising new tool in mathematical general relativity that may lead to further progress.

\section{Outlook and Remaining Challenges}

We conclude by listing the remaining challenges for the field for a complete resolution of the Spacetime Penrose Inequality without additional assumptions.

\begin{openproblem}[Unconditional Spacetime Penrose Inequality]
Prove that for any asymptotically flat initial data $(M, g, k)$ satisfying the dominant energy condition, and for any closed trapped surface $\Sigma$, the inequality
\[ M_{\mathrm{ADM}} \ge \sqrt{\frac{A(\Sigma)}{16\pi}} \]
holds \textbf{without assuming} the favorable jump condition $\tr_\Sigma k \ge 0$ or cosmic censorship.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Variational-to-Pointwise Favorable Jump Problem]
Let $\Sigma$ be a stable MOTS in initial data $(M, g, k)$ with $k \neq 0$. Suppose $\Sigma$ is selected by a variational or compactness principle among surfaces with $\theta^+ \le 0$. Determine whether one can extract the pointwise favorable jump condition $\tr_\Sigma k \ge 0$ needed by the direct Jang theorem, or produce a counterexample.
\textit{Difficulty:} High. The present manuscript does not supply such an upgrade.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Rigorous Compactness for Maximum Area Surfaces]
Establish the compactness conditions (C2) and (C3) required for the existence of a Maximum Area Trapped Surface in the general case. Specifically:
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{(C2) No loss of area to infinity:} Prove that a sequence of trapped surfaces cannot drift to infinity while maintaining large area.
    \item \textbf{(C3) No loss of area to small scales:} Prove that area cannot concentrate into small, high-curvature bubbles that vanish in the limit.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Difficulty:} Medium-High. Likely requires geometric measure theory techniques similar to those used in the existence of MOTS.
\end{openproblem}

\begin{openproblem}[Lorentzian Optimal Transport]
Develop a fully Lorentzian theory of optimal transport that avoids the need for Riemannian reduction.
\begin{itemize}
    \item Define a suitable "Lorentzian displacement convexity" for the mass functional.
    \item Prove monotonicity of this functional along geodesic flows in spacetime.
\end{itemize}
\textit{Difficulty:} Very High. This would be a fundamental breakthrough in Lorentzian geometry.
\end{openproblem}

\subsection{Roadmap to the Full Conjecture}

The full conjecture can be attacked by two logically distinct routes. The first stays on the initial-data slice and tries to complete the Jang--AMO reduction. The second works directly in spacetime and tries to justify Penrose's original area-comparison argument without hiding any global assumption.

\textbf{Route I: Complete the initial-data program.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Compactness of maximizing trapped surfaces.} Prove conditions (C2)--(C3) in Theorem~\ref{thm:MaxAreaTrapped}: no loss of maximizing sequences to infinity, no bubbling at small scales, and smooth convergence to a MOTS $\Sigma_{\max}$ with
    \[
        A(\Sigma_{\max})\ge A(\Sigma_0).
    \]
    This supplies the correct comparison surface using only the initial data.

    \item \textbf{Resolve the sign bridge.} Starting from the adjoint-cone condition of Theorem~\ref{thm:AdjointConeEuler}, prove one of the following mutually exclusive alternatives:
    \begin{enumerate}
        \item a genuine geometric calibration forcing $\tr_{\Sigma_{\max}}k\ge0$ pointwise;
        \item a sign-free replacement for the Jang corner argument that works even when $\tr_{\Sigma_{\max}}k$ changes sign;
        \item a counterexample showing that the initial-data route cannot prove the full conjecture in this form.
    \end{enumerate}
    Theorem~\ref{thm:NoGoVariational} shows that a purely scalar adjoint-inverse argument is insufficient, so this step must use additional geometry.

    \item \textbf{Run the existing direct theorem.} Once the comparison MOTS has either favorable jump or a sign-free replacement for the corner step, the current Jang--sealing--smoothing--AMO machinery gives
    \[
        M_{\ADM}(g)\ge \sqrt{A(\Sigma_{\max})/(16\pi)}
        \ge \sqrt{A(\Sigma_0)/(16\pi)}.
    \]
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Route II: Prove the Lorentzian area-comparison input.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Weak null evolution from trapped surfaces.} Construct a weak outgoing null hypersurface from any trapped surface, allowing caustics, jumps, and low regularity while preserving a meaningful area monotonicity statement.
    \item \textbf{Horizon dominance.} Prove that the resulting null evolution reaches an event-horizon cross-section $\mathcal H$ with
    \[
        A(\Sigma_0)\le A(\mathcal H).
    \]
    This is the precise missing hypothesis in Theorem~\ref{thm:HAD}.
    \item \textbf{Asymptotic mass comparison.} Combine Hawking area monotonicity, Bondi mass loss, and the Kerr bound for the final state to obtain
    \[
        M_{\ADM}\ge \sqrt{A(\mathcal H)/(16\pi)}.
    \]
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Decision tree.}
\begin{itemize}
    \item If Route I(1)--I(2a) succeeds, the conjecture follows by the present initial-data proof.
    \item If Route I(2b) succeeds, the conjecture follows after replacing only the corner-sign portion of the Jang reduction.
    \item If Route I(2c) occurs, the initial-data max-area program is not sufficient and the Lorentzian route becomes essential.
    \item If Route II succeeds, the conjecture follows in the form closest to Penrose's 1973 argument.
\end{itemize}

\textbf{Critical bottleneck.} The single most important unresolved step is the sign bridge in Route I(2). Compactness alone only provides the comparison MOTS; it does not provide the favorable jump needed by the direct Jang theorem. The Lorentzian route avoids this sign bridge but replaces it with the hard problem of proving global area comparison through weak null geometry.

\subsection{Gap-Filling Milestones}

We record the precise milestones whose proof would turn the roadmap into a proof of the conjecture. These are not additional results proved in this manuscript; they are the smallest known missing statements compatible with the arguments developed here.

\textbf{Milestone A: Compactness and regularity of the trapped-area maximizer.}
\begin{quote}
\emph{Target theorem.} Let $(M,g,k)$ be asymptotically flat, satisfy DEC, and contain a closed trapped surface $\Sigma_0$. Then the class
\[
    \mathcal T_{\le}:=\{\Sigma\subset M:\theta^+[\Sigma]\le0\}
\]
contains a smooth MOTS $\Sigma_{\max}$ maximizing area among trapped competitors in the relevant homology class, with
\[
    A(\Sigma_{\max})\ge A(\Sigma_0).
\]
\end{quote}

\textbf{Proposed proof path.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Prove a \emph{coercive enclosure theorem}: every maximizing sequence remains in a fixed compact subset of $M$. The input should combine asymptotic flatness, outer trapped barriers near infinity, and the fact that large coordinate spheres have positive outward null expansion.
    \item Prove a \emph{no-bubbling theorem}: area cannot disappear into high-curvature necks while preserving the trapped constraint. A plausible route is a curvature-scale decomposition plus the MOTS replacement theory of Andersson--Metzger and Eichmair.
    \item Pass to an integral-varifold limit, then upgrade the limit to a smooth MOTS using almost-minimizing regularity and the Euler--Lagrange condition for the constraint $\theta^+\le0$.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Consequence.} Milestone A supplies the comparison surface. It does not by itself prove the conjecture, because the Jang reduction still needs either a pointwise sign bridge or a calibrated replacement controlling the conformal mass correction.

\textbf{Milestone B: The sign bridge or a replacement for it.}
\begin{quote}
\emph{Target theorem.} For the maximizer $\Sigma_{\max}$ from Milestone A, prove one of the following:
\[
    \tr_{\Sigma_{\max}}k\ge0,
\]
or a replacement scalar-curvature mechanism for the sealed Jang metric that remains valid when $\tr_{\Sigma_{\max}}k$ changes sign.
\end{quote}

\textbf{Four viable subroutes.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Green-trace calibration.} Prove the calibration in Section~\ref{sec:GreenTraceCalibration}: the trace on \(\Sigma_{\max}\) of the ADM Green potential belongs to the admissible cone of the MOTS stability operator. Then the adjoint-cone Euler condition controls the ADM mass correction directly, without proving the pointwise sign \(\tr_{\Sigma_{\max}}k\ge0\).
    \item \textbf{Weighted flux-defect/Riccati barrier.} Prove the dual Riccati calibration in Section~\ref{subsec:DualRiccatiCalibration}: the flux defect may change sign, but its weighted pairing against the adjoint multiplier \(\zeta_\Sigma=-L_\Sigma^{-*}(\tr_\Sigma k)\) is nonnegative. By the weighted logarithmic cancellation and adjoint-collapse identity, this branch now has a precise noncircular target: prove residual dominance, a strengthened maximum-area Euler inequality, or an independent comparison principle for the ADM Green trace. The stronger pointwise inequality \(\mathfrak D_\Sigma(G_\infty)\ge0\) remains a sufficient subroute, but the weighted target is sharper because it asks only for the ADM mass-relevant sign.
    \item \textbf{Sign-free corner smoothing.} Replace the favorable-jump hypothesis in the Miao smoothing step by a compensation estimate: the negative part of the corner measure is absorbed by a bulk positive term from the Jang scalar-curvature identity, or by the calibrated conformal correction with non-increasing ADM mass.
    \item \textbf{Counterexample and rerouting.} If neither sign nor compensation holds, construct an explicit family showing that the initial-data max-area route cannot prove the full conjecture. The proof must then proceed through Milestone D below.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Consequence.} Milestones A and B together complete the initial-data proof:
\[
    M_{\ADM}(g)
    \ge \sqrt{A(\Sigma_{\max})/(16\pi)}
    \ge \sqrt{A(\Sigma_0)/(16\pi)}.
\]

\textbf{Milestone C: Equality and rigidity in the completed route.}
\begin{quote}
\emph{Target theorem.} If equality holds after Milestones A and B, then the sealed Jang metric is Schwarzschild and the original data embed in a Schwarzschild spacetime.
\end{quote}

\textbf{Proposed proof path.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Show equality forces equality in the AMO monotonicity formula, the conformal mass comparison, and the corner smoothing limit.
    \item Prove the Jang deformation has no internal bubbles and no loss of area at the comparison surface.
    \item Use the static vacuum equations and horizon regularity to invoke the Bunting--Masood-ul-Alam uniqueness theorem.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Milestone D: Lorentzian horizon dominance.}
\begin{quote}
\emph{Target theorem.} In every globally hyperbolic development satisfying the appropriate energy and completeness assumptions, any closed trapped surface $\Sigma_0$ lies in a black-hole region whose event horizon has a cross-section $\mathcal H$ satisfying
\[
    A(\Sigma_0)\le A(\mathcal H).
\]
\end{quote}

\textbf{Proposed proof path.}
\begin{enumerate}
    \item Build a weak null hypersurface from $\Sigma_0$ that survives caustics and cut loci, using a level-set or varifold formulation of null mean curvature flow.
    \item Prove lower semicontinuity of area through jumps and caustics, so trapped-surface area cannot be lost before the flow reaches the event horizon.
    \item Combine the resulting horizon dominance with Hawking area monotonicity, Bondi mass loss, and the final-state Kerr bound.
\end{enumerate}

\textbf{Consequence.} Milestone D proves the conjecture by the Lorentzian route, independently of the favorable-jump problem in the Jang reduction.

\textbf{Minimal completion principle.} A complete proof of the spacetime Penrose conjecture follows from either
\[
    \text{Milestone A}+\text{Milestone B}+\text{Milestone C},
\]
or from
\[
    \text{Milestone D}
\]
together with the standard Hawking--Kerr--Bondi comparison already isolated in Program~F. This is the current shortest roadmap exposed by the manuscript.

\textbf{Further extensions:}
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Higher dimensions:} Extend to $(n+1)$-dimensional spacetimes.
    \item \textbf{Charged black holes:} Prove $M \ge \sqrt{Q^2 + A/(16\pi)}$.
    \item \textbf{Angular momentum:} Address the Penrose inequality with rotation.
    \item \textbf{Synthetic Lorentzian geometry:} Develop TCD framework for singular spacetimes.
\end{itemize}
