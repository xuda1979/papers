# Checklist to Avoid AI-Like Content

## Structure and Formatting
- [ ] **Minimize Bullet Points**: AI models often default to lists for clarity. Prefer flowing paragraphs where possible. If a list is necessary, ensure it is integrated naturally into the text or use numbered lists for sequential steps only.
- [ ] **Avoid "Wall of Text" vs. "Listicle" Extremes**: Ensure a balance between long, dense paragraphs and fragmented lists.
- [ ] **Check Section Transitions**: Ensure sections flow logically into one another with transitional sentences, rather than abrupt jumps or generic headers.
- [ ] **No Parallel Structure Overkill**: AI loves lists where every item starts the same way (e.g., all starting with a verb, or "The X of Y" pattern). Vary the grammatical structure.

## Bullet Point Red Flags (AI Tells)
- [ ] **Avoid "Firstly/Secondly/Thirdly" Lists**: AI often enumerates mechanically. Use prose or integrate enumeration naturally.
- [ ] **No Bold Keywords at Start**: Patterns like "**Word**: explanation" are a signature AI format. Prefer inline emphasis if needed at all.
- [ ] **Avoid "Key Takeaways" Sections**: Summarizing in bullet points at end of sections is a common AI pattern.
- [ ] **Check for Symmetric Item Lengths**: AI-generated bullets often have suspiciously similar lengths. Real lists have varied item sizes.
- [ ] **No "Three-Point" Lists Without Justification**: AI defaults to three items. If you have three points, ask if you really need exactly three or if it's artificial.
- [ ] **Avoid Noun+Colon Pattern**: Items like "Efficiency: We improve...", "Accuracy: The method achieves..." scream AI.
- [ ] **No Bulleted Conclusions**: Never summarize paper contributions as bullet points in introduction or conclusion for academic papers.

## Language and Tone
- [ ] **Remove Generic Connectors**: Watch out for overuse of phrases like "In conclusion," "In summary," "It is important to note," "Furthermore," "Moreover," and "Additionally" at the start of sentences.
- [ ] **Vary Sentence Structure**: AI often produces sentences of similar length and rhythm. Mix short, punchy sentences with longer, complex ones.
- [ ] **Avoid Repetitive Phrasing**: Check for words or phrases that appear too frequently within a short span.
- [ ] **Ensure Specificity**: AI content can sometimes be vague or generic. Ensure every claim is backed by specific details, equations, or citations relevant to the specific context of the Penrose inequality and angular momentum.
- [ ] **Tone Consistency**: Ensure the academic tone is maintained throughout. AI can sometimes slip into a slightly too enthusiastic or "marketing" tone (e.g., "This revolutionary method...").
- [ ] **Avoid Hedging Stacks**: Phrases like "It could potentially be argued that perhaps..." are AI hedging. Commit to statements or omit them.

## Content and Substance
- [ ] **Verify "Common Knowledge" Statements**: AI might state obvious things as profound insights. Remove filler sentences that don't add value to the expert reader.
- [ ] **Check for "Hallucinations"**: Rigorously verify all citations and specific claims, especially if they were suggested by an AI tool.
- [ ] **Personal Voice**: Ensure the "authorial voice" is present. This often involves specific choices in terminology or a particular way of framing the problem that is unique to the authors.
- [ ] **No "As We Have Seen" Without Substance**: AI often references earlier content vaguely. Specific references are better: "From equation (2.3)..." rather than "As discussed above..."
- [ ] **Avoid Circular Definitions**: AI sometimes defines X in terms of Y, then Y in terms of X, saying nothing.

## Specific "AI Tells" to Remove
- [ ] **"Delve"**: This word is highly overrepresented in AI text.
- [ ] **"Landscape"**: (e.g., "In the landscape of general relativity...").
- [ ] **"Testament"**: (e.g., "This is a testament to...").
- [ ] **"Underscore"**: (e.g., "This underscores the importance...").
- [ ] **"Crucial/Vital/Paramount"**: Overuse of strong adjectives for emphasis where a simple statement would suffice.
- [ ] **"Pivotal"**: Another AI favorite for describing importance.
- [ ] **"Robust"**: Overused to describe methods or results.
- [ ] **"Nuanced"**: AI loves to call complex things "nuanced."
- [ ] **"Multifaceted"**: Similar to "nuanced," often empty filler.
- [ ] **"Realm"**: (e.g., "In the realm of mathematics...").
- [ ] **"Tapestry"**: (e.g., "The rich tapestry of...").
- [ ] **"Embark"**: (e.g., "We embark on an analysis...").
- [ ] **"Navigate"**: (e.g., "To navigate these challenges...").
- [ ] **"Unpack"**: (e.g., "Let us unpack this result...").
- [ ] **"Leverage"**: (e.g., "We leverage these techniques...").
- [ ] **"Foster"**: (e.g., "This fosters understanding...").
- [ ] **"Shed light on"**: Often used as filler.
- [ ] **"Pave the way"**: Cliché overused by AI.
- [ ] **"At the heart of"**: (e.g., "At the heart of our approach...").
- [ ] **"It is worth noting that"**: Usually the thing is either obvious or should just be stated directly.

## Sentence-Level Patterns to Avoid
- [ ] **"This [noun] [verb]s..."**: Sentences starting with "This approach enables...", "This result demonstrates...", "This technique allows..." in sequence.
- [ ] **"[Topic] is a [adjective] area"**: (e.g., "General relativity is a fascinating area of physics.") — empty filler.
- [ ] **"In this paper, we..."**: Overuse in introduction. One instance is fine; repeating it is AI-like.
- [ ] **"The [adjective] nature of [noun]"**: Often vacuous.
- [ ] **"By [gerund], we can [verb]"**: Overuse of this structure.

## Mathematical Writing Specific
- [ ] **Avoid "Elegant" Without Justification**: AI calls proofs "elegant" without explaining why.
- [ ] **No "Straightforward Calculation Shows"**: Either show the calculation or explain why it's omitted, but don't use this as a handwave.
- [ ] **Check Equation Introductions**: Vary how equations are introduced, not always "We have:", "This gives:", "It follows that:".
- [ ] **Avoid "Clearly/Obviously"**: What's obvious to whom? State the reasoning or trust the reader.

## Final Sanity Checks
- [ ] Read sections aloud—AI text often sounds stilted when spoken.
- [ ] Ask: "Would a human expert naturally write this sentence?"
- [ ] Check if removing a sentence loses any information—if not, it's filler.
- [ ] Ensure contractions are used consistently with the paper's style (academic papers typically avoid them).
