\section{Rigidity}\label{sec:rigidity}
%=============================================================================

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Equality Case]\label{thm:rigidity}
Assume equality holds in \eqref{eq:main}. Then the AMO monotonicity argument saturates:
\[
m_{H,J}(0)=m_{H,J}(1),
\]
so $m_{H,J}(t)$ is constant along the regular leaves of the foliation. Consequently, the non-negative rigidity terms in the monotonicity identity vanish almost everywhere; in particular
\[
R_{\tg}=0,
\qquad
\mathring{h}=0,
\qquad
\Lambda_J=0
\]
almost everywhere on the region swept out by the foliation. Since $\Lambda_J$ is continuous and non-negative, it follows that $\Lambda_J\equiv 0$ there. Hence the equality case is reduced to the external rigidity input that vanishing Kerr deviation tensor $\mathcal{S}_{(g,K)}$ characterizes Kerr initial data in the axisymmetric vacuum class. Under that additional input, equality implies that $(M,g,K)$ is a spacelike Kerr slice. Conversely, Kerr slices saturate \eqref{eq:main}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
If equality holds in \eqref{eq:main}, then by Section~\ref{sec:synthesis} the two endpoint values agree:
\[
m_{H,J}(0)=m_{H,J}(1).
\]
Theorem~\ref{thm:monotone} gives monotonicity of $m_{H,J}$ along the regular leaves, so $m_{H,J}(t)$ must be constant on that set of full measure. Therefore its derivative vanishes almost everywhere.

The derivative formula used in the monotonicity proof is a sum of non-negative contributions. Hence vanishing of the derivative forces each of those contributions to vanish almost everywhere. In particular, the scalar-curvature term vanishes:
\[
R_{\tg}=0 \qquad \text{a.e.}
\]
But by the AM-Lichnerowicz equation,
\[
R_{\tg}=\Lambda_J \phi^{-12},
\]
with $\phi>0$ and $\Lambda_J\ge 0$. Therefore $\Lambda_J=0$ almost everywhere. Since $\Lambda_J$ is continuous, it vanishes identically on the connected region covered by the foliation. Equivalently, the Kerr deviation term vanishes there.

At this point the manuscript reaches a genuine rigidity reduction: equality implies vanishing of the Kerr deviation tensor. To conclude that the original initial data is a Kerr slice, one must invoke an external initial-data rigidity theorem asserting that $\mathcal{S}_{(g,K)}=0$ characterizes Kerr in the asymptotically flat axisymmetric vacuum class. This final implication is not re-proved here.

The converse direction is unconditional: Kerr saturation was verified explicitly in Theorem~\ref{thm:kerr}.
\end{proof}

\begin{remark}[Status of the Equality Case]
The equality analysis in this section is \textbf{conditional on Theorem~\ref{thm:main}}, and hence on both the auxiliary rotating monotonicity input (H6) and the conformal mass-comparison input (H7). Subject to those inputs, the manuscript proves the reduction
\[
\text{equality in \eqref{eq:main}}
\Longrightarrow
\Lambda_J=0
\Longrightarrow
\mathcal{S}_{(g,K)}=0,
\]
but does not establish from first principles the final step
\[
\mathcal{S}_{(g,K)}=0 \Longrightarrow \text{Kerr slice}.
\]
Readers who wish to use the full ``if and only if'' characterization should therefore treat that last implication as an external rigidity input.
\end{remark}

\begin{remark}[What Was Removed]
Earlier drafts of this section claimed significantly more: derivations of stationarity from Type-D arguments, uniqueness from foliations by extremal leaves, and a direct Mars--Simon/KID characterization on the present initial-data class. Those statements were stronger than what is actually justified in the manuscript, so they have been replaced by the more modest and mathematically accurate reduction above.
\end{remark}
