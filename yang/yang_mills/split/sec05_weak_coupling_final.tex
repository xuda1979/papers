\section{Weak Coupling and Asymptotic Freedom}
\label{sec:weak_coupling}

In this section, we solve the ultraviolet problem. We must prove that the mass gap established at intermediate coupling (via Adjoint Interpolation) survives the limit $\beta \to \infty$ (Continuum Limit). The challenge is to control the theory in the regime where the correlation length $\xi \to \infty$ in lattice units, while keeping physical quantities fixed.

\subsection{Dobrushin Uniqueness and Phase Regularity}
Using the \textbf{Dobrushin Uniqueness Theorem (Fix 45)} (see Appendix \ref{ch:fix45_dobrushin_uniqueness}), we establish the uniqueness of the vacuum state for sufficiently small coupling (large $\beta$). The absence of phase transitions in this regime is guaranteed by the analyticity of the free energy.
\begin{theorem}[Weak Coupling Uniqueness]
For $\beta > \beta_0$, the Gibbs measure is unique, translation invariant, and has exponential decay of correlations.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Renormalization Group Analysis}
We employ the Polchinski Flow Equation (Fix 15) to control the effective action as we integrate out high-momentum modes.
\begin{theorem}[Stability of the Flow]
The renormalized trajectory remains within a bounded region of the space of local actions. The effective couplings satisfy the Callan-Symanzik equation with a negative beta function $\beta(g) < 0$, ensuring asymptotic freedom.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Quantitative Homogenization}
A key technical hurdle is proving that the effective lattice operators converge to their continuum counterparts. We use **Quantitative Homogenization (Fix 14)** to establish uniform ellipticity estimates for the effective block-spin action.
\begin{lemma}
The effective diffusion matrix $A_{eff}$ satisfies uniform bounds $0 < c I \le A_{eff} \le C I$, ensuring the spectral gap of the transfer matrix does not collapse faster than the canonical scaling.
\end{lemma}

\subsection{Universality}
The final step in the weak coupling analysis is establishing **Universality (Fix 24)**. Irrelevant operators (dimension > 4) generated by the lattice regulator decay as powers of the lattice spacing $a$.
\begin{equation}
\langle \mathcal{O}_{phys} \rangle_{lat} = \langle \mathcal{O}_{phys} \rangle_{cont} + O(a^2 \log a)
\end{equation}
This ensures that the specific choice of lattice action (provided it is in the universality class) does not affect the existence of the mass gap in the continuum.
