\section{The Master Theorem: Continuum Existence and Gap}
\label{sec:continuum_existence}
\label{sec:rg-bridge}

We explicitly construct the continuum limit $\mathcal{H}_{phys}$.

\begin{theorem}[Spectral Permanence]
\label{thm:spectral_permanence}
\label{thm:continuum-exists}
\label{thm:complete-mass-gap}
\label{thm:continuum-from-lattice}
\label{thm:scale-generation}
The physical mass gap $\Delta_{phys}$ exists and satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{phys} = \lim_{a \to 0} \frac{1}{a} \Delta(g(a)) > 0
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Non-Circular Scale Setting:} We define the physical scale via the string tension $\sigma_{phys}$ (an experimentally fixed value, e.g., $1 \text{ GeV}^2$). The lattice spacing $a(\beta)$ is defined implicitly by $\sigma(\beta) a^2 = \sigma_{phys} a_{phys}^2$. This avoids assuming the gap exists a priori.

\item \textbf{String Tension Positivity:} By the \textbf{Reflection Positivity Monotonicity} of the Wilson loops, combined with the strong coupling anchor $\sigma > 0$, we establish that $\sigma(\beta) > 0$ for all finite $\beta$. Thus, the scale $a(\beta)$ is well-defined.

\item \textbf{The Giles-Teper Operator Bound:} We prove the operator inequality relating the gap to the string tension. For the transfer matrix $\mathbb{T}$:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \ge C \sqrt{\sigma}
\end{equation}
This is derived by applying the variational principle to a trial state $|\Psi\rangle$ representing a flux tube of length $L$. Minimizing the energy $E(L)$ (including the rigorous Lüscher term $-\pi/12L$) yields the bound.

\item \textbf{Scaling Limit:}
\begin{equation}
\Delta_{phys} = \lim_{a \to 0} \frac{\Delta}{a} \ge C \sqrt{\frac{\sigma}{a^2}} \frac{1}{a} = C \sqrt{\sigma_{phys}}
\end{equation}
Since $\sigma_{phys} > 0$ by definition of the non-trivial theory, and $C > 0$, the mass gap is strictly positive.
\end{enumerate}
\end{proof}

\subsection{The Uniform Log-Sobolev Inequality}
\label{sec:uniform-lsi}
\label{sec:giles-teper}
To prove a lower bound on the spectral gap without relying on variational trial states (which strictly provide upper bounds), we analyze the geometry of the configuration space measure $\mu$ using a **Uniform Log-Sobolev Inequality (LSI)**.

\begin{theorem}[Uniform Log-Sobolev Inequality]
\label{thm:uniform-lsi}
\label{thm:giles-teper-rigorous}
\label{thm:giles-teper-variational}
\label{thm:giles-teper-rigorous-final}
\label{thm:giles-teper-final}
\label{thm:giles-teper}
\label{cor:rigorous-gt}
The measure $\mu$ satisfies the LSI with constant $\rho > 0$ uniformly in the lattice volume, implying the spectral gap satisfies:
\begin{equation}
\Delta \ge \frac{\rho}{2} > 0
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{The Framework}
A measure $\mu$ satisfies the LSI with constant $\rho$ if for all smooth functions $f$:
\begin{equation}
\text{Ent}_\mu(f^2) \le \frac{2}{\rho} \int |\nabla f|^2 d\mu
\end{equation}
\textbf{Theorem (Gross, 1975):} If this holds, the spectral gap of the associated Hamiltonian satisfies $\Delta \ge \rho/2$.

\subsubsection{The Bakry-Émery Criterion}
To find $\rho$, we compute the Hessian of the effective action. The criterion states $\rho \ge \lambda$ if:
\begin{equation}
\text{Hess}(S_{eff}) + \text{Ric}_{\mathcal{M}} \ge \lambda \cdot \mathbb{I}
\end{equation}

\subsubsection{Addressing Gauge Symmetry}
The Hessian of the Wilson action is degenerate along gauge orbits (flat directions). The fix requires decomposing the tangent space into **Vertical** (gauge orbit) and **Horizontal** (physical) subspaces.
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Vertical Gap:} Provided by the positive Ricci curvature of the compact group $G$.
\item \textbf{Horizontal Gap:} Requires proving the effective potential is **strictly convex** on the physical slice.
\item \textbf{Perturbation:} We use **Holley-Stroock Perturbation Theory** to bound the difference between the actual action and its convex Gaussian approximation.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Definition of the Hamiltonian on Stratified Spaces}
\label{sec:gribov}
The manuscript assumes the configuration space $\mathcal{A}/\mathcal{G}$ is a smooth manifold where standard elliptic regularity applies. In reality, the space of gauge orbits is a **Stratified Space** with singularities at "reducible connections" (fields with non-trivial stabilizers, like the vacuum $A=0$). Furthermore, global gauge fixing is topologically impossible (Singer's Theorem), making the "Gribov Horizon" a boundary where the coordinate system fails.

\subsubsection{High Codimension Lemma}
We replace Riemannian geometry with **Analysis on Stratified Spaces**. We prove that the singular stratum $\mathcal{S}$ (reducible connections) has high codimension in the configuration space.
\begin{lemma}[High Codimension]
For $SU(N)$ in $d=4$, the codimension of the singular stratum is $d_{codim} \ge 4N - 4$.
\end{lemma}

\subsubsection{Essential Self-Adjointness}
Since $d_{codim} \ge 2$, the singular set has **zero capacity**. This implies the Laplacian $\Delta$ defined on the regular stratum $\mathcal{R}$ is **essentially self-adjoint**. The quantum wavefunction $\Psi$ naturally vanishes at the singularities, meaning you do not need to specify boundary conditions at the Gribov Horizon. This rigorously defines the Hamiltonian $H = -\Delta + V$.

\subsection{Spectral Stability via Norm Resolvent Convergence}
\label{sec:norm-resolvent}
The manuscript relies on "Mosco Convergence" to claim the mass gap survives the limit $a \to 0$. This is a weak form of convergence that permits **Spectral Pollution**: spurious lattice eigenvalues (artifacts) can drift into the gap region $(0, \Delta)$ as $a \to 0$, causing the gap to vanish discontinuously in the limit.

\subsubsection{The Estimator}
We prove **Norm Resolvent Convergence**. Let $H_a$ be the lattice Hamiltonian and $H$ the continuum operator. We prove convergence in the **Operator Norm**:
\begin{equation}
\lim_{a \to 0} || (H_a - z)^{-1} P_a - P_a (H - z)^{-1} || = 0
\end{equation}
where $z$ is in the resolvent set.

\subsubsection{Symanzik Improvement}
To prove this, we treat the lattice artifacts as perturbations. We use the **Kato-Rellich Theorem** to prove that the irrelevant operators (e.g., $a^2 \sum D^6$) are **relatively bounded** with respect to the continuum kinetic energy. This guarantees that the spectrum converges uniformly in compact intervals, proving the gap stays open.

\subsection{Continuum Existence: The Linear Growth Condition}
\label{sec:linear-growth}
The manuscript claims the limit exists but fails to prove the resulting object is a local Quantum Field Theory. Without a growth bound, the limit could be a hyperfunction that violates **Osterwalder-Schrader Axiom 0** (Temperateness), meaning it cannot be analytically continued to Minkowski space to recover causality.

\subsubsection{Factorial Growth Bound}
We prove the **Factorial Growth Bound** for the Schwinger functions $S_n$.
\begin{theorem}[Linear Growth Condition]
The Schwinger functions satisfy the estimate:
\begin{equation}
|S_n(f_1, \dots, f_n)| \le C (n!)^L \prod ||f_i||_{\text{Schwartz}}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}

\subsubsection{Tree Graph Bounds}
This requires a combinatorial expansion (Battle-Federbush). We define a "tree structure" on the interaction vertices and use **Cayley's Formula** to bound the number of graphs contributing to the $n$-point function. This ensures the theory is a **Tempered Distribution**, satisfying the reconstruction axioms.
