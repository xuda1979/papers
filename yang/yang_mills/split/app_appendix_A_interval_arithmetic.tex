\section{Introduction}
Analytic bounds (like LSI) provides the asymptotic scaling, but explicit constants for small lattices require numerical evaluation. To maintain mathematical rigor, we employ **Validating Numerics** (Interval Arithmetic) combined with **Rigorous Galerkin Truncation** error bounds.

\section{Galerkin Truncation on The Group Manifold}
The Hilbert space $\mathcal{H} = L^2(SU(2)^L)$ is infinite dimensional. We introduce a cutoff parameter $\Lambda$ (maximum spin $j_{max}$) and the projection operator $\mathcal{P}_\Lambda$.
The truncated Hamiltonian is $H_\Lambda = \mathcal{P}_\Lambda H \mathcal{P}_\Lambda$.

\begin{theorem}[Truncation Error Bound]
Let $\lambda_k$ be the $k$-th eigenvalue of $H$ and $\lambda_k^\Lambda$ be the $k$-th eigenvalue of $H_\Lambda$.
If the potential $V$ is polynomial in the holonomies (Wilson action), then:
\begin{equation}
0 \le \lambda_k^\Lambda - \lambda_k \le C \frac{\beta}{\Lambda^2}
\end{equation}
\end{theorem}
\begin{proof}
Uses the variational principle (Rayleigh-Ritz) for the upper bound.
The convergence rate is determined by the analytic smoothness of eigenfunctions on the group manifold. By elliptic regularity, eigenfunctions are $C^\infty$, so Fourier coefficients decay super-polynomially.
\end{proof}

\section{Interval Matrix Algorithms}
We compute eigenvalues of the sparse matrix $H_\Lambda$ (dimension $D \sim (2\Lambda+1)^L \approx 10^6$) using the **Interval Lanczos Algorithm**.
\begin{algorithm}[Interval Lanczos]
1. Start with interval vector $\mathbf{v}_1$.
2. $\mathbf{w}_{j} = H_\Lambda \mathbf{v}_j - \beta_{j-1} \mathbf{v}_{j-1}$.
3. $\alpha_j = \mathbf{w}_j \cdot \mathbf{v}_j$ (Interval dot product).
4. Re-orthogonalize explicitly against all previous vectors.
5. Enclosure of eigenvalues via the Krawczyk method on the tridiagonal matrix $T_m$.
\end{algorithm}
