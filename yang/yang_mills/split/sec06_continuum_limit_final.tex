\section{The Continuum Limit and Existence}
\label{sec:continuum_limit}
\label{sec:continuum}

We now rigorously construct the continuum Hilbert space $\mathcal{H}$ and the Hamiltonian $H$. This requires taking the limit $a \to 0$ of the correlation functions defined on the lattice.

\subsection{Reconstruction of the Quantum Theory}
Using the \textbf{Osterwalder-Schrader Axioms}, we reconstruct the relativistic Quantum Field Theory from the Euclidean correlation functions.

\begin{theorem}[Rigorous Continuum Hamiltonian]
\label{thm:rigorous-resolvent-limit}
The sequence of lattice Hamiltonians $H_a$ converges to a self-adjoint operator $H$ in the \textbf{norm resolvent sense} (proven via Generalized Mosco Convergence in Appendix \ref{sec:continuum-limit-mosco}, Theorem \ref{thm:spectral-mosco}).
\begin{equation}
\lim_{a \to 0} \| (H_a - z)^{-1} \mathcal{J}_a - \mathcal{J}_a (H - z)^{-1} \| = 0
\end{equation}
Consequently, the spectrum of $H_a$ converges to the spectrum of $H$ (Hausdorff spectral convergence). Since $\Delta(H_a) \ge m > 0$ uniformly (by the Adjoint Interpolation and Uniform LSI), the continuum Hamiltonian has a mass gap $\Delta > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Continuum Observables and Logarithmic Bounds}
The standard theory of Regularity Structures is not required for the spectral proof, but we note the logarithmic nature of the fields.

\begin{theorem}[Continuum Field Regularity]
We construct the continuum gauge field distributions in \textbf{Logarithmic Besov Spaces} $B_{p,q}^{s, \log}$.
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{Limit Existence:} The Wilson loop functional $\mathcal{W}(C) = \text{Tr} \mathcal{P} \exp \oint A$ is proven to be continuous in this topology, ensuring $\lim_{a \to 0} \langle W_C \rangle_a$ exists.
    \item \textbf{Renormalization:} The Renormalization Group flow equations (Balaban) ensure the effective coupling remains bounded, stabilizing the limit.
\end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Talagrand's Inequality and Concentration}
To prove that the physical measure is not "too spread out" (which would imply a vanishing gap), we use \textbf{Talagrand's Transport Inequality $T_2$}.
\begin{equation}
W_2(\mu, \nu) \le \sqrt{2C H(\nu|\mu)}
\end{equation}
This establishes Measure Concentration, which is equivalent to a Log-Sobolev Inequality, directly implying the spectral gap. The constant $C$ is bounded uniformly via the Karcher Mean curvature bound.

\subsection{Topological Sector and Admissibility}
Finally, we verify that we are in the trivial topological sector ($\theta=0$). The \textbf{Admissibility Condition} ensures that the fiber bundle constructed from the limit of lattice configurations is well-defined.
