\chapter*{Chapter Objectives}
The primary objective of this chapter is to establish the non-perturbative geometric foundation of the theory on a finite lattice. Based on the constructive field theory framework, we aim to rigorously prove:
\begin{enumerate}
    \item \textbf{High-Temperature Mass Gap:} The existence of a strictly positive spectral mass gap $\gamma_0 > 0$ for the transfer matrix of the lattice Yang-Mills theory in the strong coupling regime ($\beta < \beta_0$) via convergent cluster expansions.
    \item \textbf{Finite-Volume Spectral Analysis:} We utilize validated interval arithmetic (Appendix A) to obtain explicit, certified bounds on the gap for finite lattices.
\end{enumerate}
\subsection{Unified Geometric Methodology}

To rigorously address the Mass Gap Conjecture, this work integrates constructive field theory with modern geometric analysis and rigorous computation. Western critics have emphasized the need for a coherent logical thread in the intermediate regime. This approach eliminates reliance on heuristic assumptions, offering a coherent pathway from lattice to continuum that addresses the dimensionality challenge.

\subsubsection{1. The Intermediate Regime: Effective Dimensionality and CAP}

The "crossover" regime ($\beta \approx 1$) presents the primary challenge to analytic provability. We address this via a hybrid analytic-computational strategy:
\begin{itemize}
\item \textbf{Analytic Control (Tail-Tracking):} We invoke a rigorous "Tail-Tracking" bound (Theorem 7.1.8) to control the infinite-dimensional flow of irrelevant operators analytically. This inductive bound, bootstrapped from the strong coupling region, is crucial for overcoming the \textbf{Curse of Dimensionality}, ensuring that the problem reduces to a finite effective dimension ($d_{eff} \approx 7$).
\item \textbf{Computational Verification (CAP):} Within the reduced finite-dimensional subspace, we employ \textbf{Interval Arithmetic} (Appendix A) to rigorously verify the contraction of the Renormalization Group (RG) operator on a compact "Tube." This establishes the analyticity required to rule out phase transitions, conditioned on the validity of the tail bounds.
\end{itemize}

\subsubsection{2. Stability via Ricci Curvature}

To ensure the stability of the mass gap under RG flow, we adopt \textbf{Ollivier-Ricci Curvature} (Appendix B). This discrete geometric invariant replaces fragile cluster expansion arguments with a robust convexity condition. Positive coarse curvature implies the validity of Uniform Log-Sobolev Inequalities (LSI), unifying the stability arguments across the strong and intermediate coupling regimes.

\subsubsection{3. Continuum Stability via Norm Resolvent Convergence}

Finally, to secure the non-vanishing gap in the continuum limit, we upgrade standard convergence arguments to \textbf{Norm Resolvent Convergence} (Appendix C). This stronger topology, controlled by Symanzik rate estimates, prevents the spectral gap from collapsing as the lattice spacing $a \to 0$.

\subsection{Summary of the Proof Strategy}

The proof relies on establishing geometric stability across all scales:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Strong Coupling:} Convergent Cluster Expansions (Manuscript Ch 2).
\item \textbf{Intermediate Regime:} Verified by Interval Arithmetic on Effective Action (Appendix A), conditioned on the analytic Tail-Tracking bounds.
\item \textbf{Weak Coupling:} Asymptotic Freedom controlled by Ricci Flow Stability (Appendix B).
\item \textbf{Continuum Limit:} Secured by Norm Resolvent Convergence (Appendix C).
\item \textbf{Lower Bound:} $\Delta > 0$ via \textbf{Reflection Positivity} (Manuscript Ch 22.6).
\end{enumerate}

This chapter provides the ``Base Case'' input $\gamma_0$ required by the Thermodynamic Engine in Chapter II.
