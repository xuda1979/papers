\section{Conditional Mass Gap via Adjoint Interpolation}
\label{sec:adjoint_interpolation}

This chapter details a conditional proof of the mass gap existence by interpolating between \(\mathcal{N}=1\) Super Yang-Mills (SYM) and pure Yang-Mills theory. We formulate the argument as a theorem contingent on a specific non-phase-transition hypothesis.

\subsection{The Interpolating Action}

\begin{definition}[Interpolating Action]
We consider the action \(S(M)\) connecting the SYM action (\(M=0\)) to the pure Yang-Mills action (\(M\to\infty\)) via a massive adjoint Majorana fermion (gluino):
\begin{equation}
S(M) = S_{YM} + S_{Majorana}(M) = \int \text{Tr}\left( \frac{1}{4} F_{\mu\nu}^2 + \bar{\lambda}( \not D + M ) \lambda \right) d^4x
\end{equation}
\end{definition}

\subsection{Endpoint Analysis}

\begin{proposition}[Starting Point Gap]
For \(M=0\), the theory is \(\mathcal{N}=1\) SYM. The Witten Index \(\text{Tr}(-1)^F \neq 0\) for \(SU(N)\) implies that supersymmetry is unbroken, and the spectrum has a mass gap \(\Delta(0) > 0\).
\end{proposition}

\subsection{Gap Transport and Phase Stability}

The central difficulty is ensuring the gap remains open during the deformation \(M: 0 \to \infty\). We rely on the preservation of Center Symmetry to rule out confinement-deconfinement transitions, and formulate the absence of first-order "liquid-gas" transitions as a precise hypothesis.

\begin{hypothesis}[$H_{Analytic}$: Regularity of Free Energy]
\label{hyp:analytic}
The free energy density \(f(M) = - \lim_{V\to\infty} \frac{1}{V} \log Z(M)\) is real analytic for \(M \in [0, \infty)\), and the correlation length \(\xi(M)\) remains finite for all finite \(M\).
\end{hypothesis}

\begin{theorem}[Conditional Mass Gap]
Assume Hypothesis \ref{hyp:analytic}. Then the mass gap \(\Delta(M) > 0\) for all \(M \in [0, \infty]\). In particular, pure Yang-Mills theory (\(M \to \infty\)) exhibits a mass gap.
\begin{proof}
By the Proposition, \(\Delta(0) > 0\). The Center Symmetry \(Z_N\) is preserved by the adjoint matter for all \(M\), preventing a symmetry-breaking transition. Under Hypothesis \ref{hyp:analytic}, no first-order singularity occurs. Thus, the gapped phase at \(M=0\) is adiabatically connected to the limit \(M \to \infty\).
\end{proof}
\end{theorem}
