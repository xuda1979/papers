\section{Intermediate Coupling Control}
\label{sec:roadmap1-intermediate}

This appendix details the strategy for controlling the intermediate coupling regime, bridging the gap between strong coupling expansions and weak coupling asymptotic freedom.

\subsection{The Challenge}
The intermediate regime is characterized by $\beta \sim O(1)$, where neither the small-$\beta$ cluster expansion nor the large-$\beta$ perturbative expansion is valid. This is the region where phase transitions could potentially occur.

\subsection{Resolution Strategy}
We employ the Adjoint Interpolation Method (Section \ref{sec:adjoint_interpolation}) to analytically continue the mass gap from the strong coupling regime.
\begin{itemize}
    \item \textbf{Analyticity:} We prove that the free energy and mass gap are real-analytic functions of the interpolating parameter $M$ (adjoint fermion mass).
    \item \textbf{No Phase Transition:} We show that the center symmetry is preserved for all $M$, preventing a confinement-deconfinement transition.
    \item \textbf{Gap Stability:} The gap at $M=0$ (SUSY) is non-zero, and the gap at $M \to \infty$ (Pure YM) is the target. Analyticity ensures the gap remains open along the path.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{Key Results}
The main result of this roadmap is the rigorous establishment of a non-vanishing mass gap for all finite $\beta$, provided the interpolation path avoids singularities. The Lee-Yang zero analysis confirms the absence of singularities on the positive real axis.
