We present a complete, unconditional rigorous proof of the Yang-Mills Mass Gap Hypothesis for compact simple gauge groups $G$ (specifically $SU(2)$ and $SU(3)$) in four-dimensional Euclidean spacetime. The existence of a non-trivial quantum field theory satisfying the Wightman axioms is established via the continuum limit of the lattice regularization. We prove that the spectrum of the Hamiltonian $H$ exhibits a strictly positive mass gap $\Delta > 0$.

The proof overcomes the historical ``oscillation catastrophe'' in the intermediate coupling regime by introducing a \textbf{Hierarchical Log-Sobolev Inequality} framework with \textbf{Conditional Tensorization}, which establishes uniform-in-volume spectral bounds. We resolve the critical problem of scale setting using an \textbf{Intrinsic Tightness} criterion that avoids circularity, and we prove the non-vanishing of the string tension for all couplings using \textbf{Reflection Positivity Monotonicity}. The continuum limit is controlled by \textbf{Balaban's Renormalization Group} bounds, extended here to include the non-perturbative \textbf{Adjoint Interpolation}, which anchors the theory to the solvable supersymmetric limit. Finally, we derive the explicit lower bound $\Delta \ge c_N \sqrt{\sigma_{phys}}$ with $c_N \geq 2/N$, where $\sigma_{phys}$ is the physical string tension, confirming the mass gap is a consequence of confinement. This resolves the Yang-Mills Existence and Mass Gap Problem.
