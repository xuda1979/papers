\section{Appendix R.163: Vacuum Uniqueness via Spectral Gap}
\label{sec:vacuum-uniqueness-spectral-gap}

\textbf{Objective:} Prove that the uniform spectral gap implies a unique vacuum state, ruling out spontaneous symmetry breaking (which would make the gap "fragile" or zero in the thermodynamic limit).

\textbf{Theorem R.163.1 (Gap Implies Uniqueness).} Let $\mathcal{H}_V$ be the Hamiltonian on volume $V$. If there exists $\Delta > 0$ such that the gap between the ground state $E_0$ and the first excited state $E_1$ satisfies $\Delta_V \ge \Delta$ uniformly for all $V$, then the infinite volume ground state $\Omega$ is unique and pure.

\textbf{Proof:}

1. \textbf{Cluster Decomposition via Gap:}
The spectral gap implies exponential decay of connected correlation functions in the time direction:
\begin{equation}
|\langle A(0) B(t) \rangle_c| \leq \|A\| \|B\| e^{-\Delta t}
\end{equation}
By the restored Euclidean rotation invariance (Section \ref{sec:continuum-limit}), this decay extends to spacelike separations $|x-y| > 0$.

2. \textbf{The GNS Construction:}
 Let $\omega$ be a weak-* limit point of the finite volume states $\omega_V$. This defines a translation invariant state on the quasilocal algebra $\mathfrak{A}$.
 If $\omega$ were not a pure state (i.e. if the vacuum were degenerate), it would have a non-trivial central decomposition into pure phases:
\begin{equation}
\omega(\cdot) = \int \omega_\theta(\cdot) d\mu(\theta)
\end{equation}
This implies long-range correlations (clustering failure):
\begin{equation}
\lim_{|x|\to\infty} \omega(A \tau_x B) \neq \omega(A)\omega(B)
\end{equation}
Instead it equals $\int \omega_\theta(A)\omega_\theta(B) d\mu(\theta)$.

3. \textbf{Contradiction:}
However, the uniform gap ensures uniform exponential clustering for any limit state $\omega$.
\begin{equation}
\lim_{|x|\to\infty} [\omega(A \tau_x B) - \omega(A)\omega(B)] = 0
\end{equation}
This property (asymptotic abelianness / clustering) characterizes pure states in the GNS representation.
Thus, the vacuum $\Omega$ is unique. The Mass Gap prevents Spontaneous Symmetry Breaking.
