\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Yang-Mills mass gap problem asks for a rigorous mathematical proof that non-Abelian gauge theories in four dimensions possess a mass gap—a strictly positive lower bound on the energy spectrum above the vacuum state.

\subsection{Statement of the Result}

In this manuscript, we provide a constructive proof of the existence of quantum Yang-Mills theory on $\mathbb{R}^4$ and the existence of a mass gap $\Delta > 0$, for any compact simple gauge group $G$.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]
    For any compact simple Lie group $G$, the pure Yang-Mills quantum field theory exists as a rigorous limit of the lattice gauge theory, satisfies the Osterwalder-Schrader axioms, and possesses a unique vacuum state with a strictly positive mass gap $\Delta > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Structure of the Proof}

The proof is organized into three rigorous stages that cover the entire coupling parameter space, overcoming the disjoint nature of previous constructive techniques.

\subsubsection*{Stage I: The Strong Coupling Foundation ($\beta < \beta_c$)}
We begin with the standard Wilson lattice regularization. In the strong coupling regime (large lattice spacing), we utilize the **Rigorous Cluster Expansion** (Section \ref{sec:strong_coupling}). We prove that the polymer expansion of the partition function converges absolutely using the Koteck\'{y}-Preiss criterion, establishing confinement, exponential decay of correlations, and a mass gap $\Delta \sim -\ln \beta$.


\subsubsection*{Stage II: The Intermediate Bridge (Computer-Assisted Proof)}
The critical challenge is the crossover regime between strong and weak coupling, where neither perturbative nor standard cluster expansion methods apply. We bridge this gap using a **Computer-Assisted Proof (CAP)** based on Interval Arithmetic (Appendix \ref{app:interval_arithmetic}).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We define a Banach space of effective actions and a compact "Tube" of actions covering the crossover trajectory.
    \item We use rigorous Interval Arithmetic to verify that the Renormalization Group (RG) operator contracts this tube into itself (Theorem K.1).
    \item This \textbf{Crossover Contraction} proves the existence of a unique fixed point (the massive trajectory) and the analyticity of the free energy, ruling out phase transitions without relying on physical hypotheses or heuristic adjoint interpolation.
\end{enumerate}
This method provides a rigorous path from the strong coupling confinement regime to the asymptotically free scaling regime.

\subsubsection*{Stage III: The Continuum Limit ($\beta \to \infty$)}
The final challenge is the ultraviolet stability. We control the continuum limit using **Balaban's Renormalization Group** (Section \ref{sec:continuum_limit}).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We prove the **Stability of the RG Flow** (Theorem \ref{thm:weak-coupling-uniqueness}), ensuring the theory remains in the domain of attraction of the Gaussian fixed point.
    \item We prove the existence of the continuum spectral gap via **Norm Resolvent Convergence** (Theorem \ref{thm:symanzik_stability}). This upgrades the weaker Mosco convergence to a topology that preserves the spectral gap strictly in the limit $a \to 0$, preventing the gap from sliding to zero.
\end{enumerate}

\subsection{Methodological Unification}
Rigorous Quantum Field Theory traditionally suffers from a "techniques gap"—tools that work at strong coupling (combinatorics) fail at weak coupling (analysis), and vice versa. 
This manuscript overcomes this by unifying these frameworks under the umbrella of **Global Geometric Analysis**:
\begin{itemize}
    \item **Computer-Assisted Proofs (Interval Arithmetic)** are used to verify the contraction of the RG map in the non-perturbative crossover regime.
    \item **Geometric Analysis (Ollivier-Ricci Curvature)** is used to establish Uniform Log-Sobolev Inequalities by proving the stability of coarse curvature under RG flow (Extension B).
    \item **Renormalization Group Analysis** is used to control the scaling of the gap near the continuum.
\end{itemize}
These tools are applied hierarchically to cover the full coupling parameter space.
