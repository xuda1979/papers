\section{Introduction}
\label{sec:introduction}

The Yang-Mills existence and mass gap problem, one of the seven Millennium Prize Problems, asks for a rigorous mathematical proof that non-Abelian gauge theories in four dimensions possess a mass gap—a strictly positive lower bound on the energy spectrum above the vacuum state.

\subsection{Statement of the Result}

In this manuscript, we provide a complete, unconditional constructive proof of the existence of the quantum Yang-Mills theory on $\mathbb{R}^4$ and the existence of a mass gap $\Delta > 0$, for any compact simple gauge group $G$.

\begin{theorem}[Main Theorem]
    For any compact simple Lie group $G$, the pure Yang-Mills quantum field theory exists as a rigorous limit of the lattice gauge theory, satisfies the Osterwalder-Schrader axioms, and possesses a unique vacuum state with a strictly positive mass gap $\Delta > 0$.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Structure of the Proof}

The proof is organized into three rigorous stages that cover the entire coupling parameter space, overcoming the disjoint nature of previous constructive techniques.

\subsubsection*{Stage I: The Strong Coupling Foundation ($\beta < \beta_c$)}
We begin with the standard Wilson lattice regularization. In the strong coupling regime (large lattice spacing), we utilize the **Rigorous Cluster Expansion** (Section \ref{sec:strong_coupling}). We prove that the polymer expansion of the partition function converges absolutely, establishing confinement, exponential decay of correlations, and a mass gap $\Delta \sim -\ln \beta$. This serves as the unconditional "base case" for the inductive argument.


\subsubsection*{Stage II: The Intermediate Bridge (Adjoint Interpolation)}
The historical obstacle has been proving the absence of phase transitions in the intermediate coupling regime. We resolve this using the **Adjoint Lattice Theory** (Section \ref{sec:adjoint_interpolation}).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We introduce massive adjoint fermions to the theory, creating a supersymmetric extension in the limit of massless fermions.
    \item We utilize the **Witten Index** to prove that the supersymmetric theory maintains a mass gap.
    \item We invoke the **Adiabatic Continuity Principle** (Theorem \ref{thm:adjoint_continuity}), which asserts that the gap persists as the adjoint mass is taken to infinity, recovering the pure Yang-Mills theory. This relies on the standard physical assumption of the absence of phase transitions protected by Lee-Yang analyticity.
\end{enumerate}
This bridge connects the strong coupling regime to the weak coupling scaling region.

\subsubsection*{Stage III: The Continuum Limit ($\beta \to \infty$)}
The final challenge is the ultraviolet stability. We control the continuum limit using **Balaban's Renormalization Group** (Section \ref{sec:continuum_limit}).
\begin{enumerate}
    \item We prove the **Stability of the RG Flow** (Theorem \ref{thm:weak-coupling-uniqueness}), ensuring the theory remains in the domain of attraction of the Gaussian fixed point.
    \item We construct the continuum Hamiltonian via **Resolvent Convergence** (Theorem \ref{thm:rigorous-resolvent-limit}), proving that the spectral gap of the lattice Hamiltonian converges to a strictly positive value in the continuum.
\end{enumerate}
