\section{Formal Framework}

\begin{definition}[Space of Effective Actions]
Let $\mathcal{B}$ be the Banach space of gauge-invariant lattice actions $S$ defined on the unit hypercube, equipped with the weighted norm:
\begin{equation}
    \|S\|_w = \sup_{\phi} \frac{|S(\phi)|}{\|\phi\|^2 + 1}
\end{equation}
where the supremum is over bounded lattice fields.
\end{definition}

\begin{definition}[The Crossover Tube]
\label{def:tube_strict}
The Tube $\mathcal{T} \subset \mathcal{B}$ is defined as the union of a finite set of balls in the projected relevant subspace $\mathcal{V}_{rel} \cong \mathbb{R}^{d_{rel}}$ ($d_{rel}=7$), combined with a uniform Sobolev-type bound on the infinite-dimensional irrelevant tail.
\[
\mathcal{T} = \bigcup_{i=1}^N \left\{ S \in \mathcal{B} : \|\mathcal{P}_{rel} S - c_i\| \le r_i, \, \|\mathcal{P}_{irr} S\|_{tail} \le \epsilon_{tail} \right\}
\]
where $\mathcal{P}_{rel}$ is the projection onto the relevant coupling directions, and $\|\cdot\|_{tail}$ is a norm ensuring strong decay of coefficients (e.g., exponential spatial decay and power-law derivative decay).
\end{definition}

\begin{lemma}[Compactness of the Tube]
\label{lem:tube_compactness}
The Tube $\mathcal{T}$ defined above is compact in the weighted norm topology $\|\cdot\|_w$ of $\mathcal{B}$.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
For infinite dimensional Banach spaces, closed and bounded sets are not necessarily compact. We must establish **total boundedness**.
The set $\mathcal{T}$ is the sum of a finite dimensional compact set (the projected balls) and an infinite dimensional tail set.
The tail constraint $\|\mathcal{P}_{irr} S\|_{tail} \le \epsilon_{tail}$ involves a stronger norm than the global weighted norm $\|\cdot\|_w$ (which only requires boundedness). Specifically, the tail norm enforces exponential decay of the kernel $K(x-y)$ with rate $m > 0$.
By the Rellich-Kondrachov type compactness theorems for lattice systems, the embedding of the space of exponentially decaying interactions into the space of bounded interactions is compact. 
Explicitly: for any $\epsilon > 0$, we can choose a finite lattice volume $V_\epsilon$ and a finite truncation of the operator expansion such that the remainder in $\|\cdot\|_w$ is less than $\epsilon/2$ uniformly for all $S$ in the tail set. The finite-dimensional truncation is totally bounded.
Thus, $\mathcal{T}$ admits a finite $\epsilon$-net for any $\epsilon$, making it compact.
\end{proof}
