\subsection{Overview}

The intermediate coupling regime $\beta \in [\beta_S, \beta_W]$ lies outside the domain of both cluster expansion (strong coupling) and perturbation theory (weak coupling). We establish the absence of phase transitions in this regime using a Computer-Assisted Proof (CAP) based on rigorous interval arithmetic.

\begin{theorem}[Tube Contraction]
\label{thm:tube_contraction_cap}
There exists a compact set $\mathcal{T} \subset \mathcal{B}$ of gauge-invariant effective actions such that the renormalization group map $\mathcal{R}$ satisfies
\[
\mathcal{R}(\mathcal{T}) \subset \mathrm{int}(\mathcal{T}).
\]
This strict inclusion holds for all $\beta \in [\VerBetaIntermediateMin, \VerBetaIntermediateMax]$, with the flow preserving all discrete symmetries of the lattice action.
\end{theorem}

The verification establishes three properties:
\begin{enumerate}
\item \textbf{Contraction:} The RG flow maps the tube strictly into its interior.
\item \textbf{Symmetry Preservation:} The block-spin kernel preserves $H_4 \times P \times C$ at each step.
\item \textbf{Seamless Handoff:} The CAP range $[\VerBetaIntermediateMin, \VerBetaIntermediateMax]$ connects exactly to the strong-coupling regime ($\beta \le \VerBetaStrongMax$) and the perturbative regime ($\beta \ge \VerBetaWeakMin$).
\end{enumerate}

\subsubsection{Action Space and Operator Basis}

We parameterize gauge-invariant lattice actions by their expansion in local operators:
\begin{equation}
\mathcal{S}[U] = \sum_{i=1}^{N_{\mathrm{ops}}} c_i \mathcal{O}_i[U],
\end{equation}
where $\{\mathcal{O}_i\}$ are gauge-invariant operators ordered by scaling dimension.

\subsubsection{Forbidden Operators}

Dimension-5 operators are rigorously forbidden by the symmetries of the lattice action.

\begin{lemma}[Dimension-5 Exclusion]
\label{lem:dim5_forbidden}
Under the combined symmetry group $H_4 \times P \times C$, no gauge-invariant local operators of dimension 5 exist.
\end{lemma}

\begin{proof}
The unique dimension-5 candidate is the topological density. This operator is parity-odd and is therefore excluded by the parity invariance of the Wilson action.
\end{proof}
