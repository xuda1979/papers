# Critical audit of the Yang--Mills mass-gap proof (2026-07-06)

## Verdict

**The repository does NOT prove the Clay Yang--Mills mass gap conjecture.**
The chain app192 → app193 → app200 → app202 → app208 contains a tautological
core: the lattice mass gap `delta = 0.05` is a free parameter chosen at
instantiation time, not derived from the Yang--Mills action. Every number in
the chain

delta = 0.05 → gap_disc = 0.05 → gap_cont_lower = 0.028 → m_lat ≥ 0.024
→ gap_cont ≥ 0.02395117 → m_phys ≥ 0.15476169

descends from this free choice. The "theorem" reduces to: *if* the Ward--Hodge
operator has a positive spectral gap (which is true by construction), *then*
the continuum transport yields a positive mass gap. The antecedent is
tautological, so the consequent carries no information about Yang--Mills theory.

This audit documents the gap precisely, fixes the most egregious technical
errors, and states honestly what would be needed to close the proof.

## The tautological core

### The Ward--Hodge weight and its saddle-point vanishing

The Ward--Hodge harmonic weight (app192, Eq. ward_hodge_weight) is

  a_p*(theta) = (beta/3) sqrt((9 - |tr W_p(theta)|^2) / 6).

This is the SU(3) trace-plane modulus: the magnitude of the component of W_p
perpendicular to the scalar direction in the Hilbert--Schmidt metric.

**At the Wilson action saddle point W_p = I (the identity), |tr W_p| = 3, so
a_p*(theta_saddle) = 0.** The Ward--Hodge operator K_W = M_T^T A M_T vanishes
at the saddle. The spectral gap is 0.

### The canonical instantiation inverts the physical logic

app192 §Canonical chart-box instantiation chooses

  a_* := (1 - delta) / lambda_max(M_T^T M_T)

for a free parameter delta in (0,1), then computes r_* from a_*. This makes
rho(K_W(theta*)) = 1 - delta by construction. The "mass gap" is delta, chosen
by fiat.

The proof works for *any* delta in (0,1). Setting delta = 0.5 gives mass gap
0.5; setting delta = 0.01 gives mass gap 0.99. The choice delta = 0.05 is
unmotivated by the Yang--Mills action.

### The Wilson action is nowhere in the chain

The Ward weights a_p(theta) are stubbed at the uniform SU(3) bound [-beta, beta]
in ward_weighted_spectral_carving.py. The "instantiation" replaces this with a
single chosen value a_* at a single chosen point theta*. At no point does the
proof:

  1. Compute the Yang--Mills equilibrium measure on the 2x2 block.
  2. Evaluate E[a_p*(theta)] or any moment of a_p* under this measure.
  3. Show that the measure is concentrated where a_p* is bounded away from 0.
  4. Connect the spectrum of K_W(theta*) to the transfer matrix / Hamiltonian.

Without (1)-(4), the number delta = 0.05 has no Yang--Mills content.

## Additional technical errors

### E1. BMWR mass-gap identification (app208, thm:bmwr, step 5)

The proof asserts:

  "The Ward--Hodge operator K_W is the mass operator of Yang--Mills: its
  smallest nonzero eigenvalue is the squared mass gap m^2."

This is false as stated. K_W = M_T^T A M_T is a 4x4 matrix acting on the
coordinate-current space of the 2x2 plaquette block. The Yang--Mills mass gap
is the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of the Hamiltonian H_cont acting on the
physical Hilbert space H. No theorem connects these two spectra.

The Källén--Lehmann representation written in the proof is also wrong: in 4D
Euclidean space the two-point function is

  W_2(x,y) = int_0^infty rho(m^2) (m K_1(m|x-y|)) / (4 pi^2 |x-y|) dm^2,

not int rho(m^2) e^{-m|x-y|} dm^2 (the latter is the 2D massive propagator).

The identification m_phys = sqrt(gap_cont) is unjustified. Even if gap_cont
were a genuine Yang--Mills spectral gap, it would already be a mass-squared
quantity in the standard KL conventions, and the physical mass would be
sqrt(gap_cont) only if the KL spectral density starts at gap_cont = m^2; but
this requires the missing identification theorem.

### E2. RPHK half-space factorization (app208, thm:rphk)

The proof claims the heat kernel "factorizes on the half-spaces":
S_t^(a) = S_t^(a,+) S_t^(a,-). This is not what reflection positivity
requires. RP requires <F, theta F> >= 0 for F in the future-positive subspace,
where theta is the time reflection. The factorization S_t = S_t^+ S_t^- does
not hold for a generic heat kernel; what holds is the reflection-invariance
[theta, S_t] = 0 and the Markov property on the half-space.

The numerical verification in continuum_atlas_transport_theorem.py checks
[S_t, theta] = 0 (true, since theta is an involution commuting with Delta_1)
and a "future/past part bound" that is not the RP condition. The RP condition
is a positivity statement about a quadratic form, not a norm bound on
components of S_t f.

### E3. WHCHK finite-complex mismatch

The honesty note in app208 claims the WHCHK is verified on a finite complex
with n_0 = 6, n_1 = 12. The actual verification uses n_0 = 4, n_1 = 4, n_2 = 0
(four plaquettes, four coordinate currents, no 2-forms). The Hodge-decomposition
preservation claims are checked on this 4-dimensional complex, not the claimed
12-dimensional one. With n_2 = 0, the "coexact preservation" test is trivial.

### E4. SGTMM uses no actual lattice data

The "spectral-gap transport via min--max" (app208, thm:sgtmm) computes
gap_a = gap_disc - 2*C*a^2 at five lattice spacings, but gap_disc is the
constant 0.024 (imported from LATTICE_MASS_GAP_LOWER), not a重新-computed
spectral gap at each spacing. The "Richardson extrapolation" verifies that
gap_disc - 2*C*a^2 converges to gap_disc as a -> 0, which is tautological
(the formula is gap_disc minus a known function of a).

### E5. The "lattice mass gap" 0.024 is not a lattice computation

The value 0.024 comes from:
  gap_disc = delta = 0.05 (chosen)
  gap_cont_lower = 0.05 - 0.02 - 0.002 = 0.028 (subtracting sheaf coboundary
    bounds that are themselves proportional to a_lattice * delta)
  m_lower = 0.028 - 0.004 = 0.024 (APIC contraction-radius subtraction)

No step computes a spectral gap from the Yang--Mills Wilson action on a finite
lattice. The "lattice mass gap" is an algebraic function of the chosen delta.

## What would be needed to close the proof

The honest path to a Clay-level proof requires, at minimum:

### R1. Derive the Ward--Hodge gap from the Wilson action

Compute (or rigorously bound) the equilibrium expectation of a_p*(theta) under
the 2x2-block Wilson action

  d mu_beta(U) proportional to exp(beta sum_p Re tr W_p) prod_p dU_p.

Specifically, show that for beta >= beta_c (some critical coupling) the measure
is concentrated on configurations where |tr W_p| <= r_max(beta) < 3, giving

  a_* = (beta/3) sqrt((9 - r_max^2)/6) >= a_min(beta) > 0,

and hence rho(K_W) <= 1 - delta(beta) with delta(beta) > 0 derived, not
chosen. The Gross--Witten--Wadia transition at beta = N/2 = 3/2 for SU(3) is
the natural critical scale.

### R2. Connect K_W spectrum to the transfer matrix

Prove a theorem of the form: the smallest nonzero eigenvalue of K_W on the
2x2 block bounds the spectral gap of the lattice transfer matrix T_lat (and
hence the Hamiltonian H_lat = -log T_lat / a_lat). This requires:

  (a) Identifying K_W as a factor in the transfer matrix (a Ward identity).
  (b) Showing the gap of K_W controls the gap of T_lat (a spectral mapping
      theorem or a variational argument).

Without (a)+(b), the spectrum of K_W has no physical mass-gap content.

### R3. Continuum limit with uniform atlas Lipschitz constants

Prove that the atlas Lipschitz constants Lip(g_st) are uniformly bounded as
a -> 0. The current proof uses Lip(g_st) computed on a 2x2 block at a fixed
a_lattice = 0.1; the a -> 0 limit is not controlled.

### R4. OS positivity on the convergent side

Verify reflection positivity of the limiting continuum Schwinger functions.
The current RPHK argument checks [theta, S_t] = 0, which is necessary but not
sufficient for RP.

### R5. Wightman reconstruction with mass-gap transport

Close the gap between "continuum Schwinger functions exist and satisfy OS"
and "the reconstructed Wightman Hamiltonian has a positive mass gap." This
requires showing the KL spectral density rho(m^2) is supported on
[m_phys^2, infty) with m_phys > 0, which requires R2.

## Conclusion

The repository contains a non-trivial finite-complex calculation (the
Ward--Hodge decomposition on the 2x2 plaquette block with the unimodular M_T)
and a well-defined conditional transport theorem. The conditional's hypothesis
("K_W has a positive gap") is true by construction at a non-saddle point, but
the connection to the Yang--Mills action and to the physical Hamiltonian is
not established.

**The Clay Yang--Mills mass gap conjecture is NOT proved by this repository.**

The honest next step is R1: derive the Ward--Hodge gap from the Wilson action
via the equilibrium measure on the 2x2 block. This is a concrete, attackable
problem (single-plaquette SU(3) integrals are known analytically). Until R1
is done, the rest of the chain is conditional on an proved hypothesis.

## New finding (2026-07-06): fundamental physical obstruction

A Monte Carlo computation (`verfification/ward_hodge_gap_obstruction.py`,
results in `verfification/ward_hodge_gap_obstruction_results.json`)
reveals a **fundamental physical obstruction** to the Ward--Hodge
approach, beyond the tautological-core issue.

### The obstruction

The Ward--Hodge weight
  a_p*(theta) = (beta / (3*sqrt(6))) * sqrt(9 - |tr W_p(theta)|^2)

is evaluated on configurations theta drawn from the 2x2-block Wilson
equilibrium measure mu_beta. At weak coupling (large beta), the measure
concentrates near W_p = I, with
  9 - |tr W_p|^2 ~ c/beta,  c ≈ 20 (from MC),
so
  a_p* ~ sqrt(c*beta) / (3*sqrt(6)) ~ 0.61 * sqrt(beta).

The spectral radius of K_W = M_T^T A M_T scales as
  rho(K_W) ~ a_p* * lambda_max(M_T^T M_T) ~ 0.61 * 2.618 * sqrt(beta)
           ~ 1.59 * sqrt(beta),
which exceeds 1 for beta >~ 0.4. The "gap" 1 - rho(K_W) becomes **negative**.

### Monte Carlo confirmation

Sampling 300 configurations of the 2x2-block Wilson action (4 independent
plaquettes, exp(beta * Re tr W_p)) and computing rho(K_W) for each:

  beta | E[|trW|^2] | E[rho(KW)] | P(gap > 0) | E[gap | gap > 0]
  -----+------------+------------+------------+------------------
  0.5  |   1.078    |   0.515    |   1.000    |   0.486
  1.0  |   1.260    |   1.022    |   0.197    |   0.028
  1.5  |   1.539    |   1.510    |   0.000    |   (nan)
  2.0  |   1.950    |   1.973    |   0.000    |   (nan)
  3.0  |   3.152    |   2.757    |   0.000    |   (nan)
  5.0  |   4.913    |   3.851    |   0.000    |   (nan)

For beta >= 1.5, the Ward--Hodge operator has spectral radius > 1 for
**every** sampled configuration. The "mass gap" 1 - rho(K_W) is negative.

### Implication

The Ward--Hodge construction **cannot** produce a positive mass gap in the
continuum limit (beta -> infinity). The proof's positive value delta = 0.05
is obtained by evaluating K_W at a non-typical configuration theta* with
|tr W_p(theta*)| = 1.375, which has probability essentially zero under the
Wilson measure at any beta >= 1.0.

This is not a technical gap that can be closed by better estimates. It is a
physical mismatch: the Ward--Hodge weight grows with beta, while the
construction requires it bounded by 1/lambda_max(MtM) ~ 0.38. The
construction is only valid at strong coupling (beta <~ 0.4), which is the
opposite of the continuum regime.

### Path forward

The Ward--Hodge weight a_p* needs to be **renormalized** to have a
well-defined continuum limit. The natural renormalization is to divide by
the typical scale sqrt(beta), giving a renormalized weight
  a_p*^{ren} = a_p* / sqrt(beta) = (sqrt(beta)/(3*sqrt(6))) * sqrt(9 - |tr W_p|^2).
But then a_p*^{ren} ~ sqrt(c)/(3*sqrt(6)) ~ 0.61 in the continuum limit, and
rho(K_W^{ren}) ~ 0.61 * 2.618 ~ 1.59, still > 1. A further renormalization
(by 1/lambda_max(MtM)) gives rho(K_W^{ren'}) ~ 1, which is the critical
value — the gap goes to zero.

This suggests the Ward--Hodge operator, as defined, captures the **total
fluctuation** of the plaquette, not the mass gap. The mass gap is a
different spectral quantity (e.g., the transfer-matrix gap, or the
exponential decay rate of the Wilson loop correlation), and the
Ward--Hodge approach does not isolate it.

**Conclusion: the Ward--Hodge approach, as currently formulated, cannot
prove the Yang--Mills mass gap conjecture. A different spectral
identification is needed.**

## Update 2026-07-07 (second pass): PCTO errors found and corrected; new unconditional theorem added

A further critical re-audit of the 2026-07-07 PCTO fix (app209) found that
**the PCTO fix itself contained two new errors**, on top of the residual
open hypothesis (H) that it correctly flagged as proved:

### E6. MITT (Theorem `mitt`, app209) step (2) is false

The proof asserted "$T_{OS}$ preserves $\mathcal{A}_{plaq}$" without
justification. This is false in general: the Wilson action couples every
plaquette to its neighbors through shared links, so the conditional
expectation $\Pi = E[\cdot \mid \mathcal{A}_{plaq}]$ onto the 4-plaquette
block sub-algebra does **not** commute with the transfer matrix except in
the degenerate case where the $2\times2$ block is the entire lattice (no
neighbors to integrate out). $T_{OS}$ maps a plaquette observable to a
function of the boundary links of the whole time-slice, not just the four
plaquettes of the block, so $T_{OS}\mathcal{A}_{plaq}\not\subseteq
\mathcal{A}_{plaq}$ in general.

### E7. Theorem `clay_main_H` (app209) step (4) uses an inapplicable formula

The proof substituted the strong-coupling mass formula
$m_{lat}^2(\beta,N) = -2\log\tanh(\beta/N)$ directly into the continuum-limit
ratio $m_{lat}^2(\beta)/a(\beta)^2$ and claimed it tends to a finite positive
constant under hypothesis (H). This is not merely proved but **uses a
formula outside its domain of validity**: substituting the two-loop
asymptotic-freedom lattice spacing $a(\beta)$, the ratio
$m^{SC}(\beta,N)^2/a(\beta)^2 \to +\infty$ as $\beta\to\infty$ (verified both
analytically and numerically; see `split/app210_perron_frobenius_mass_gap.tex`,
Lemma `sc_diverges`), because $m^{SC}$ decays like $e^{-4\beta/N}$ while
$a(\beta)^2$ decays like $e^{-2b_0\beta}$ with $2b_0 = \tfrac{11}{3}N \gg 4/N$.
The strong-coupling formula is a genuine theorem only for $\beta < \beta_c(N)$;
extrapolating it to $\beta\to\infty$ is illegitimate.

### R6. New unconditional theorem: finite-lattice mass gap via Krein-Rutman

To replace the flawed MITT/PCTO spectral identification, a genuinely new
appendix `split/app210_perron_frobenius_mass_gap.tex` proves an
**unconditional** finite-lattice mass gap theorem using the Krein-Rutman
theorem (the infinite-dimensional generalization of Perron-Frobenius) applied
directly to the Osterwalder-Schrader transfer matrix $T_{OS}$ of Luscher
(1977):

- The transfer-matrix kernel $K(U,U')$ is strictly positive for every
  $\beta > 0$ (immediate from the Wilson action being bounded and the Haar
  measure being positive).
- $T_{OS}$ is compact (continuous kernel on a compact configuration space)
  and strongly positive.
- By Krein-Rutman, the leading eigenvalue $\lambda_0 = 1$ (the vacuum) is
  **simple**, and every other eigenvalue satisfies $|\lambda_n| < 1$.
- Hence `gap(Lambda, beta) > 0` for **every** finite lattice $\Lambda$,
  every $N \ge 2$, and every $\beta > 0$ -- with **no free parameters** and
  **no restriction to strong coupling**.

This is combined with the (pre-existing, correctly cited) Osterwalder-Seiler
strong-coupling cluster-expansion bound to get a **uniform** (volume-
independent) mass gap at $\beta < \beta_c(N)$.

A numerical verification (`verfification/perron_frobenius_mass_gap.py`,
14 passing tests in `verfification/test_perron_frobenius_mass_gap.py`)
instantiates this construction concretely on a class-function reduction of a
single SU(2) plaquette, confirming strict kernel positivity, eigenvalue
simplicity, and positive spectral gap for beta up to 500 (deep weak coupling)
-- in sharp contrast to the Ward-Hodge construction, whose spectral radius
was shown (2026-07-06 Monte Carlo) to exceed 1 for beta >= 1.5.

### Corrected hypothesis (H')

`split/app210_perron_frobenius_mass_gap.tex` (Theorem `corrected_H`) restates
the continuum-limit hypothesis correctly: it must involve the **actual**
non-perturbatively-defined lattice mass gap $m(\beta) = \inf
\operatorname{spec}(H_{lat}(\beta))\setminus\{0\}$, not the strong-coupling
extrapolation $m^{SC}(\beta)$. The hypothesis is

  (H') `lim_{beta -> infinity} m(beta)/a(beta) = m_phys in (0, infinity)`.

This is, honestly, exactly the Clay Millennium Prize problem restated. No
formula for $m(\beta)$ at weak coupling is known in closed form; that is
precisely the open problem.
