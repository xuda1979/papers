       Conservative Finite--Volume Schemes as Sequence Models}

\begin{abstract}
  We introduce \emph{Discrete Token--Flow Networks} (DTFNs), a new family
  of neural sequence architectures whose forward pass is a
  \emph{conservative finite--volume scheme} for a hyperbolic
  conservation law on a one--dimensional token grid.  Each token is
  represented as a non--negative mass vector, and information is
  propagated by learned numerical fluxes between neighbouring positions.
  Three structural invariants are preserved by construction at every
  forward pass: (i) \emph{exact} mass conservation, (ii) pointwise
  non--negativity, and (iii) a discrete maximum principle under the
  Courant--Friedrichs--Lewy (CFL) condition.  We introduce eight
  instantiations of the framework---a baseline upwind DTFN, a
  bidirectional variant, a higher--order MUSCL scheme with Van~Leer
  slope limiting, a full variant combining all three with a
  learnable source term, a fifth--order WENO--Z scheme, an adaptive
  per--position CFL variant, a conservation--preserving sparse
  attention, and a novel \emph{Flux--Corrected Transport} (FCT)
  variant that blends low-- and high--order fluxes via a learned
  limiter---and prove that the flux--only variants satisfy the three
  invariants exactly, while the remaining variants satisfy them up to
  controlled, characterised residuals.
  We additionally prove (a) an
  \emph{$L^1$--contraction / adversarial stability} theorem showing
  that DTFN--U and --B are $1$--Lipschitz in the input, so no
  $\ell_\infty$--bounded adversarial perturbation can grow under the
  forward pass; (b) a \emph{CFL spectral--radius stability
  certificate} that gives a learnable, per--layer stability guarantee;
  (c) a \emph{continuous--time (neural PDE) limit} showing that as
  $K \to \infty$ the DTFN trajectory converges to the entropy solution
  of a neural scalar conservation law; (d) a \emph{universal
  approximation theorem} for local translation--equivariant sequence
  maps, and (e) a \emph{DTFN--SSM bridge} showing that a DTFN
  linearised around a constant equilibrium recovers a discrete
  state--space model whose top eigenvalue is exactly $1$ (mass
  conservation).  Empirically, DTFNs achieve $100\%$ token and
  sequence accuracy on Dyck--1 across five random seeds and on Dyck--2
  (single seed), whereas comparably--sized LSTMs, Transformers, and an
  S4 (S4D) baseline stall at $54$--$87\%$ token accuracy and $0\%$
  sequence accuracy under the same training budget.  DTFNs perfectly
  extrapolate from training lengths
  $4$--$20$ to test lengths up to $100$ bracket pairs (a $5\times$
  length gap) with no accuracy degradation, while baselines lose
  $10$--$13$ percentage points over the same range.  At sequence length
  $4096$, DTFN--U is $3.3\times$ faster than a two--layer Transformer
  and $1.6\times$ faster than an LSTM.  We position DTFNs as the first
  application of conservative finite--volume methods to natural
  language sequence modeling and demonstrate that numerical--analysis
  structure can be a substrate for inductive biases that are both
  provably correct and empirically effective.
\end{abstract}

\section{Introduction}

Transformer--based models with self--attention
\cite{vaswani2017attention} underpin most state--of--the--art language
models but incur $\mathcal{O}(L^2)$ complexity and memory in sequence
length $L$, limiting their efficiency on long contexts.  Recent
alternatives such as structured state--space models (SSMs)
\cite{gu2022combining,gu2022efficiently} reduce complexity yet still rely
on implicit global convolutions or linear recurrences.  Concurrently,
neural ordinary and partial differential equation models
\cite{raissi2019physics,mordvintsev2020growing} use continuous--time
dynamics to propagate features but target physical systems rather than
discrete token sequences.

Our goal is to embed the stability and conservation principles from
finite--volume solvers directly into a sequence backbone for language
modeling.  This perspective bridges numerical analysis and deep
learning: instead of building upon attention or convolution, we adopt
discrete conservation laws from hyperbolic PDEs to design a neural
architecture whose dynamics are physically interpretable.  By doing so
we move beyond merely local updates (as in recurrent networks,
state--space models, or neural cellular automata) and imbue the model
with exact conservation and positivity constraints that guarantee
stability irrespective of sequence length.

\paragraph{Contributions.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item We introduce \emph{Discrete Token--Flow Networks} (DTFNs),
        sequence models whose forward pass is a conservative
        finite--volume scheme on a 1D token grid (Section~\ref{sec:method}).
  \item We instantiate eight variants---upwind (DTFN--U),
        bidirectional (DTFN--B), higher--order MUSCL with Van~Leer
        limiting (DTFN--MUSCL), a full variant (DTFN--FULL) combining
        bidirectional MUSCL with a learnable source term, a
        fifth--order WENO--Z reconstruction (DTFN--WENO5), an adaptive
        per--position CFL variant (DTFN--Adaptive), a
        conservation--preserving sparse attention variant
        (DTFN--CPSA), and a novel \emph{Flux--Corrected Transport}
        variant (DTFN--FCT) that adaptively blends low-- and
        high--order fluxes via a \emph{learned} Zalesak limiter---and
        prove that the flux--only variants satisfy \emph{exact} mass
        conservation, pointwise non--negativity, and a discrete
        maximum principle, while the remaining variants satisfy them
        up to controlled, characterised residuals
        (Theorems~\ref{thm:weno5}--\ref{thm:fct}
        and \ref{thm:conservation}--\ref{thm:maxprinciple}).
  \item We prove a TVD (total--variation--diminishing) bound for
        DTFN--MUSCL (Theorem~\ref{thm:tvd}) and a length--extrapolation
        guarantee (Theorem~\ref{thm:extrap}) showing that DTFNs
        transfer exactly to longer sequences than seen at training.
  \item We prove an \emph{$L^1$--contraction / adversarial stability}
        theorem (Theorem~\ref{thm:l1}) showing that DTFN--U and --B
        are $1$--Lipschitz in the input mass: no $\ell_\infty$--bounded
        adversarial perturbation can grow under the DTFN forward pass.
        We additionally prove a \emph{CFL spectral--radius stability
        certificate} (Theorem~\ref{thm:cfl}) that gives a learnable,
        per--layer stability guarantee, and we verify both empirically
        against random and FGSM adversarial perturbations
        (Section~\ref{sec:stability-exp}).
  \item We prove a \emph{continuous--time (neural PDE) limit}
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:pde}) showing that as $K \to \infty$ the
        DTFN mass trajectory converges to the entropy solution of a
        neural scalar conservation law $\partial_t p + \partial_x
        F(p;\theta) = 0$, and a \emph{universal approximation theorem}
        for local translation--equivariant sequence maps
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:univ}).
  \item We demonstrate empirically that DTFNs achieve $100\%$
        accuracy on Dyck--1 across five random seeds and on Dyck--2
        (single seed), outperforming LSTM, Transformer, and S4 (S4D)
        baselines by $\sim 40$ percentage points on Dyck--1
        extrapolation
        (Section~\ref{sec:experiments}).  DTFNs extrapolate to
        sequences $5\times$ longer than training with no degradation,
        and DTFN--U is $3.3\times$ faster than a two--layer
        Transformer at length $4096$.  We also ablate the FluxNet
        width and depth, showing the results are insensitive to a
        $16\times$ range of capacities (Section~\ref{sec:exp-ablation-fluxnet}).

        (Section~\ref{sec:ssm-bridge}): linearising the DTFN update
        around a constant equilibrium recovers a discrete state--space
        model $z^{k+1} = T z^k$ whose spectral structure (top
        eigenvalue $= 1$ for mass conservation, contraction rate $<
        1$ for stability) we verify empirically: the top eigenvalue
        matches to within $2\times 10^{-3}$ and the contraction rate
        to within $0.09$.
  \item We demonstrate that \emph{adaptive per--position CFL}
        (DTFN--Adaptive) is a sample--efficient variant that solves
        Dyck--1 at $99.78\%$ accuracy with half the hidden width
        required by fixed--CFL variants, and we validate all
        variants with wall--clock scaling to $16{,}384$ tokens.
  \item We introduce \emph{FCT--DTFN}, a neural adaptation of
        classical Flux--Corrected Transport (Boris \& Book 1973;
        Zalesak 1979), in which a \emph{learned} limiter blends
        monotone upwind and fifth--order WENO5 fluxes.  FCT--DTFN
        achieves order $\approx 3$ on smooth advection with an
        analytic limiter, and inherits exact conservation and
        positivity by construction (Theorem~\ref{thm:fct}).  To
        our knowledge this is the first application of FCT to
        neural sequence models.
  \item We release a self--contained PyTorch implementation under a
        permissive license, with full experiment scripts and seeds.
\end{itemize}

\section{Related work}

\paragraph{Self--attention and SSMs.}
Self--attention \cite{vaswani2017attention} revolutionised sequence
modeling but suffers from quadratic complexity.  State--space models
\cite{gu2022combining,gu2022efficiently} achieve linear scaling via
structured convolutions and recurrence, offering a compelling
alternative but still relying on implicit global interactions.  More
recent SSMs refine this picture: S4D \cite{gu2022s4d} simplifies the
S4 parameterisation while preserving its HiPPO--based spectral
structure; S5 \cite{smith2023s5} parallelises the state recursion via
MIMO dynamics; Mamba \cite{gu2023mamba} replaces the fixed S4 kernel
with a \emph{selective} (input--dependent) state space, closing much of
the gap to attention on language tasks; and RWKV \cite{peng2023rwkv}
reformulates attention--free recurrence as a linearised
Transformer--RNN hybrid.  Section~\ref{sec:ssm-theory} shows that DTFNs
linearise to a conservative SSM near a constant equilibrium, providing
an explicit bridge between finite--volume schemes and this family.
None of these families enforces conservation or positivity constraints.

\paragraph{Neural differential equations.}
Neural ODEs/PDEs \cite{raissi2019physics,mordvintsev2020growing} treat
continuous fields with learned dynamics; however, these methods target
physical systems rather than discrete token sequences.  Neural cellular
automata \cite{mordvintsev2020growing} produce rich behaviour from
local rules but lack explicit conservation laws.

\paragraph{Neural finite--volume methods.}
(U)NFV \cite{lichtle2023unfv} introduces neural finite--volume solvers
for hyperbolic PDEs with learned fluxes on \emph{continuous} domains.
In contrast, DTFNs discretise the domain into \emph{token positions}
and embed finite--volume conservation directly into sequence backbones
to ensure stability and interpretability for language modeling.  To
our knowledge, DTFNs are the first application of finite--volume
conservation laws to natural language sequence modeling.

\paragraph{Recurrent and convolutional baselines.}
Classical RNNs and WaveNet \cite{oord2016wavenet} achieve linear--time
complexity by sharing parameters across positions, but they do not
enforce any conservation properties and are susceptible to exploding
or vanishing gradients.  DTFNs inherit the $\mathcal{O}(L)$ complexity
of these models while adding provable conservation and stability.

\section{Method}
\label{sec:method}

\subsection{Setup and notation}

Figure~\ref{fig:architecture} gives a visual overview of the
framework.  We consider a sequence of $L$ tokens with embedding dimension $d$.
At each discrete time $k\in\{0,\dots,K-1\}$, let
$p^k(i)\in\Rplus^d$ denote the non--negative \emph{mass vector}
associated with position $i\in\{0,\dots,L-1\}$.  We update $p$ according
to a one--dimensional finite--volume scheme over $K$ iterations:
\begin{equation}
  p^{k+1}(i) \;\leftarrow\; p^k(i) + \lambda\,\bigl(F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2}\bigr) + \sigma\, S_i,
  \label{eq:update}
\end{equation}
where $\lambda\in(0,1)$ is a learnable CFL coefficient, $F_{i\pm1/2}\in\Rplus^d$
are learned \emph{numerical fluxes} representing mass flow across
interfaces, and $S_i$ is an optional learned source term with scale
$\sigma\ge 0$.  We take $\Delta x = 1$ without loss of generality,
so $\lambda = \Delta t / \Delta x = \Delta t$.

\paragraph{Flux network.}
The flux $F_{i+1/2}= \FluxNet_\theta\bigl(p^k(i), p^k(i+1)\bigr)$ is
constrained to output non--negative vectors via a final Softplus
activation.  We use two parameterisations:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{FluxNet}: an MLP
        $\mathrm{Softplus}\bigl(W_2\,\tanh(W_1[p_i;p_{i+1}]+b_1)+b_2\bigr)$
        with a residual upwind baseline $v\,p_i$ added, where
        $v=\mathrm{Softplus}(v_\text{logit})$ is a learnable scalar
        velocity.  The residual form ensures the network initialises
        close to the classical upwind scheme.
  \item \textbf{MonotoneFluxNet}: same MLP but with the
        left--input weight matrix $W_\text{left}$ replaced by
        $|W_\text{left}|$, which is a sufficient condition for the
        flux to be monotone non--decreasing in $p_i$.  Monotonicity in
        the left input implies the scheme is monotone and hence TVD
        (Theorem~\ref{thm:tvd}).
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Source network.}
The source $S_i = \SourceNet_\phi(p^k(i))$ is parameterised as
$\mathrm{Softplus}(W\,p_i + b)$ scaled by a learnable positive scalar.
In \emph{conservative mode}, we subtract the mean across positions:
$S_i \leftarrow S_i - \tfrac{1}{L}\sum_j S_j$, which ensures
$\sum_i S_i = 0$ and thus preserves total mass exactly.  In
\emph{non--conservative mode}, the source can inject mass at a
bounded rate, which is useful for language modelling where tokens may
appear or disappear.

\paragraph{Boundary conditions.}
We use \emph{zero--flux} (reflecting) boundary conditions:
$F_{-1/2} = F_{L-1/2} = 0$.  No mass enters or leaves the domain.

\subsection{DTFN variants}

We instantiate eight variants of the DTFN framework, spanning
first-- to fifth--order spatial accuracy, fixed and adaptive time
stepping, and local and non--local coupling:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{DTFN--U} (upwind): unidirectional, FluxNet, no source.
        First--order in space, $\mathcal{O}(\lambda)$ local truncation
        error.
  \item \textbf{DTFN--B} (bidirectional): two coupled stacks in
        opposite directions, combined by a per--channel convex gate
        $\alpha\odot m_\text{fwd} + (1-\alpha)\odot m_\text{bwd}$,
        which preserves total mass when each stack does.
  \item \textbf{DTFN--MUSCL}: higher--order scheme with Van~Leer slope
        limiter (Section~\ref{sec:muscl}) and MonotoneFluxNet.
        Second--order in smooth regions, TVD near discontinuities.
  \item \textbf{DTFN--FULL}: bidirectional + MUSCL + non--conservative
        source + MLP decoder.  The most expressive variant; the source
        term allows net mass creation/destruction, so conservation
        holds only up to the source magnitude.
  \item \textbf{DTFN--WENO5}: fifth--order WENO--Z reconstruction
        \cite{borges2008improved} in smooth regions with the
        essentially non--oscillatory property near discontinuities
        (Section~\ref{sec:weno5}).  Achieves $\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^5)$
        truncation error while preserving conservation and positivity.
  \item \textbf{DTFN--Adaptive}: per--position, per--channel learnable
        CFL coefficient $\lambda_i \in (0,1)$ (Section~\ref{sec:adaptive}).
        Allows DTFN to allocate larger time steps in smooth regions
        and smaller steps near sharp gradients, recovering
        solution--adaptive time integration.
  \item \textbf{DTFN--CPSA}: Conservation--Preserving Sparse
        Attention (Section~\ref{sec:cpsa}).  Extends the receptive
        field beyond the local stencil via a windowed attention
        mechanism whose attention weights are constrained to sum to
        one, preserving total mass while enabling $\mathcal{O}(L\log L)$
        non--local coupling.
  \item \textbf{DTFN--FCT}: Flux--Corrected Transport
        (Section~\ref{sec:fct}).  Adaptively blends a monotone
        low--order (upwind) flux and a fifth--order WENO5 high--order
        flux via a \emph{learned} Zalesak limiter, achieving
        high--order accuracy in smooth regions while preserving
        positivity and conservation near discontinuities.
\end{itemize}

\subsection{MUSCL higher--order scheme}
\label{sec:muscl}

To achieve second--order accuracy in smooth regions while remaining
non--oscillatory near discontinuities, we use the MUSCL approach
\cite{vanleer1979towards}: reconstruct piecewise--linear profiles in
each cell using slope--limited gradients
\[
  \sigma_i = \mathrm{limiter}\bigl(p_i - p_{i-1},\; p_{i+1} - p_i\bigr),
\]
then evaluate the flux at the left and right reconstructed face values.
We use the Van~Leer limiter
\[
  \mathrm{van\_leer}(a,b) = \begin{cases}
    \dfrac{2ab}{a+b} & \text{if } ab > 0,\\[2pt]
    0 & \text{otherwise.}
  \end{cases}
\]
The left and right face values are
\[
  p^\text{L}_{i+1/2} = p_i + \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_i,
  \qquad
  p^\text{R}_{i+1/2} = p_{i+1} - \tfrac{1}{2}\sigma_{i+1},
\]
and the flux is $F_{i+1/2} = \FluxNet_\theta(p^\text{L}_{i+1/2}, p^\text{R}_{i+1/2})$.

\subsection{Positivity--preserving step}

To guarantee pointwise non--negativity \emph{and} exact conservation
in the presence of learned fluxes (which may violate monotonicity
during training), we use a \emph{flux--scaling} positivity step:
\[
  \lambda_\text{safe} = \min\!\Bigl(\lambda,\;\min_{i: \mathrm{d}m_i<0}\;\tfrac{p^k_i - \Eps}{-\mathrm{d}m_i}\Bigr),
  \qquad
  p^{k+1} = p^k + \lambda_\text{safe}\,\mathrm{d}m,
\]
where $\mathrm{d}m_i = F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2} + \sigma S_i$ and $\Eps>0$ is a
small floor.  This ensures $p^{k+1}\ge \Eps$ without adding or removing
mass.  When the CFL condition is satisfied and the scheme is monotone,
$\lambda_\text{safe}=\lambda$ and the step is a no--op.

\subsection{WENO--Z fifth--order reconstruction}
\label{sec:weno5}

To achieve fifth--order accuracy in smooth regions while preserving
the essentially non--oscillatory (ENO) property near discontinuities,
we employ the WENO--Z reconstruction of \cite{borges2008improved}.
Given three candidate stencils $\{p_0, p_1, p_2\}$ centred at cell
$i$, the WENO--Z reconstruction at face $i+1/2$ is
\[
  p_{i+1/2} = \sum_{k=0}^{2} \omega_k\, p_k,
  \qquad
  \omega_k = \frac{\alpha_k}{\sum_j \alpha_j},
  \qquad
  \alpha_k = d_k\!\left(1 + \frac{\tau_5}{\beta_k + \Eps}\right),
\]
where $d_k = \{1/10, 6/10, 3/10\}$ are the ideal weights, $\beta_k$
are smoothness indicators, $\tau_5 = |\beta_2 - \beta_0|$ is the
WENO--Z nonlinear weight, and $\Eps = 10^{-6}$ prevents division by
zero.  The flux is then $F_{i+1/2} = v\, p_{i+1/2}$ and the update
remains $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda(F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2})$.
Because WENO--Z is a convex combination of conservative stencil
fluxes, the scheme inherits \emph{exact conservation} and, with the
flux--scaling positivity step, pointwise non--negativity.

\begin{theorem}[WENO5 order]
  \label{thm:weno5}
  In smooth regions away from critical points, the WENO--Z
  reconstruction satisfies $|p_{i+1/2} - p(x_{i+1/2})| =
  \mathcal{O}(\Delta x^5)$, so the DTFN--WENO5 update has local
  truncation error $\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^5)$.  Near discontinuities
  the scheme degenerates to first--order upwind, preserving
  non--oscillatory behaviour.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Adaptive per--position CFL}
\label{sec:adaptive}

The base DTFN uses a single learnable scalar $\lambda$ per layer,
shared across all positions and channels.  In many sequence tasks the
relevant time scale varies along the sequence: positions near sharp
transitions (e.g.\ bracket boundaries in Dyck) require smaller time
steps for stability, while smooth interior regions tolerate larger
steps.  We therefore introduce a \emph{learned per--position CFL
field} $\lambda_i = \sigma(\ell_i) \in (0,1)$, where $\ell_i$ is
produced by a small MLP from the local mass vector:
\[
  \lambda_i = \sigma\!\bigl(W_2\, \mathrm{ReLU}(W_1 p^k_i + b_1) + b_2\bigr),
  \qquad i = 0, \dots, L-1.
\]
The update becomes $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda_i (F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2})$.
Because each $\lambda_i \in (0,1)$, the CFL condition holds
pointwise.  The scheme conserves total mass exactly when all
$\lambda_i$ are equal (the usual case); when $\lambda_i$ varies
across positions, the flux--weighted sum leaves a residual
$\sum_i (\lambda_{i+1} - \lambda_i) F_{i+1/2}$ bounded by
$\sum_i |F_{i+1/2}|$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive}).  With the
positivity step the scheme remains non--negative.  The additional
cost is one MLP evaluation per cell per step, preserving
$\mathcal{O}(L)$ complexity.

\begin{theorem}[Adaptive conservation]
  \label{thm:adaptive}
  If each $\lambda_i \in [0,1]$, the adaptive update
  $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda_i (F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2})$ conserves
  total mass:
  $\sum_i p^{k+1}_i = \sum_i p^k_i + \sum_i \lambda_i(F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2})$.
  The flux--weighted sum telescopes: grouping terms by face,
  $\sum_i \lambda_i F_{i-1/2} - \sum_i \lambda_i F_{i+1/2} = \sum_i
  (\lambda_{i+1} - \lambda_i) F_{i+1/2}$, which vanishes when
  $\lambda_i$ is constant and is bounded by $\sum_i |F_{i+1/2}|$ in
  general.  With the flux--scaling positivity step the scheme remains
  non--negative.
\end{theorem}

\subsection{Conservation--Preserving Sparse Attention}
\label{sec:cpsa}

The local stencil of DTFN--U/MUSCL/WENO5 limits the receptive field
to $K$ cells after $K$ layers.  For tasks requiring long--range
dependencies, we introduce \emph{Conservation--Preserving Sparse
Attention} (CPSA): a windowed attention mechanism whose attention
weights are constrained to sum to one per query, so that the
attention output is a convex combination of the value mass vectors
and total mass is preserved.  Concretely, for query $q_i$ and keys
$\{k_j\}$ over a window $[i-w, i+w]$:
\[
  \alpha_{ij} = \frac{\exp(q_i^\top k_j / \sqrt{d})}{\sum_{j' \in [i-w,i+w]} \exp(q_i^\top k_{j'} / \sqrt{d})},
  \qquad
  \tilde p_i = \sum_{j \in [i-w,i+w]} \alpha_{ij}\, p_j.
\]
Because $\sum_j \alpha_{ij} = 1$ and $\alpha_{ij} \ge 0$, the output
$\tilde p_i$ is a convex combination of the $\{p_j\}$, so
$\sum_i \tilde p_i = \sum_j p_j$ when the windows tile the sequence
with doubly stochastic coverage.  Near the sequence boundaries the
windows are truncated, so conservation holds exactly only in the
interior; we restore global conservation by padding with a
zero--contribution boundary mask that renormalises the attention
weights over the available window, giving
$\sum_i \tilde p_i = \sum_j p_j + \mathcal{O}(w \,\|p\|_\infty / L)$,
i.e.\ a boundary--controlled residual that vanishes as $L \gg w$.
In float32 the softmax normalisation adds a further $\sim 10^{-5}$
round--off (Table~\ref{tab:conservation}).  The attention output is
then composed with the local DTFN update, yielding a hybrid
local/non--local scheme with $\mathcal{O}(L w)$ complexity for window
size $w$.

\subsection{Flux--Corrected Transport DTFN (FCT--DTFN)}
\label{sec:fct}

\newcommand{\Flow}{F_{\mathrm{low}}}
\newcommand{\Fhigh}{F_{\mathrm{high}}}

The schemes above face a fundamental trade--off: \emph{low--order}
upwind fluxes are monotone and positivity--preserving but only
first--order accurate; \emph{high--order} WENO5 fluxes achieve
fifth--order accuracy in smooth regions but can produce spurious
oscillations near discontinuities (Godunov's theorem
\cite{godunov1959difference}).  We resolve
this with a neural adaptation of \emph{Flux--Corrected Transport}
(FCT) \cite{boris1973fct,zalesak1979fct}, a classical idea from
computational fluid dynamics that, to our knowledge, has not been
applied to neural sequence models.

\paragraph{FCT principle.}
Given a low--order flux $\Flow$ and a high--order flux $\Fhigh$, define
the \emph{anti--diffusive correction} $A = \Fhigh - \Flow$.  The FCT
flux is
\begin{equation}
  F_{\mathrm{FCT}} \;=\; \Flow \;+\; \phi \odot A,
  \label{eq:fct}
\end{equation}
where $\phi \in [0,1]^{(L-1)\times d}$ is a \emph{limiter} that
decides, per face and per channel, how much high--order correction to
apply.  Setting $\phi = 0$ recovers the monotone low--order scheme;
$\phi = 1$ recovers the high--order scheme.

\paragraph{Learned Zalesak limiter.}
Classical FCT uses a hand--coded limiter (Zalesak 1979) based on
geometric constraints.  We replace this with a \emph{learned} limiter
$\phi = \mathrm{LimiterNet}_\psi(s)$, where $s$ is a feature vector
containing the Jameson smoothness indicator
\cite{jameson1995analysis},
\begin{equation}
  \sigma_i \;=\; \frac{|p_{i+1} - 2 p_i + p_{i-1}|}
                    {|p_{i+1}| + 2|p_i| + |p_{i-1}| + \varepsilon}
  \;\in\; [0,1],
\end{equation}
together with $p_i, p_{i+1}$, first and second differences, and the
two adjacent smoothness values.  $\mathrm{LimiterNet}_\psi$ is a
3--layer MLP with SiLU activations and a final sigmoid, outputting
$\phi \in (0,1)$.

\paragraph{Theoretical guarantees.}
FCT--DTFN inherits the structural properties of DTFN:
\begin{theorem}[FCT conservation and positivity]
  \label{thm:fct}
  Let $\Flow$ be a conservative flux and $A = \Fhigh - \Flow$ a flux
  difference.  Then for any $\phi \in [0,1]^{(L-1)\times d}$:
  \begin{enumerate}
    \item[(a)] \textbf{Exact conservation:}
      $\sum_i p^{k+1}_i = \sum_i p^k_i$, since both $\Flow$ and $A$
      telescope across the domain.
    \item[(b)] \textbf{Positivity:} If the low--order update
      $p_{\mathrm{low}} = p + \lambda(\Flow^{\mathrm{in}} -
      \Flow^{\mathrm{out}}) \ge 0$ and the limiter satisfies the
      Zalesak constraint
      $\phi_{i+1/2} \le \min\!\bigl(
        \frac{p_{\max,i} - p_{\mathrm{low},i}}{A^{\mathrm{in}}_i - A^{\mathrm{out}}_i + \varepsilon},
        \frac{p_{\mathrm{low},i} - p_{\min,i}}{A^{\mathrm{out}}_i - A^{\mathrm{in}}_i + \varepsilon}
      \bigr)$
      for cells where the correction would push $p$ outside
      $[p_{\min}, p_{\max}]$, then $p^{k+1} \ge 0$.
    \item[(c)] \textbf{High--order recovery:} In smooth regions where
      $\phi \to 1$, $F_{\mathrm{FCT}} = \Fhigh$ and the scheme
      achieves the full order of $\Fhigh$ (5th order for WENO5).
  \end{enumerate}
\end{theorem}
The Zalesak constraint is enforced as a hard safety bound on top of
the learned $\phi$ (see Appendix~\ref{app:fct-proof}).

\paragraph{Why FCT--DTFN is novel.}
Three distinctions from prior work: (i) FCT has not been applied to
neural sequence models; (ii) the limiter is \emph{learned} rather than
hand--coded, allowing the model to discover task--specific
smoothness/accuracy trade--offs; (iii) conservation and positivity are
guaranteed \emph{by construction}, regardless of the learned limiter
output.

\subsection{Forward pass}

Algorithm~\ref{alg:dtfn} summarises the forward pass of DTFN--U.  The
other variants are obtained by replacing the flux computation (MUSCL)
and/or running a parallel backward stack and merging (bidirectional).

\begin{algorithm}[t]
  \caption{DTFN--U forward pass.  All operations are differentiable;
    $\FluxNet_\theta$ has non--negative output by Softplus, and the
    positivity step ensures $p\ge\Eps$ without breaking conservation.}
  \label{alg:dtfn}
  \begin{algorithmic}[1]
    \Require token sequence $x\in\{0,\dots,V-1\}^L$, embedding $E\in\R^{V\times d}$,
    decoder $W_d\in\R^{d\times V}$, $K$ update steps, $\Eps>0$.
    \State $m \gets \mathrm{Softplus}(E\cdot \mathrm{Emb}(x)) + \Eps$ \Comment{initial non--negative mass}
    \State $\lambda \gets \mathrm{sigmoid}(\alpha)$ \Comment{CFL coefficient in $(0,1)$}
    \For{$k \gets 1$ to $K$}
      \State $F \gets \FluxNet_\theta(m_{:,i}, m_{:,i+1})$ for $i=0$ to $L-2$ \Comment{compute fluxes}
      \State $\mathrm{dm} \gets 0$
      \State $\mathrm{dm}_{:,0} \gets -F_{:,0}$, $\mathrm{dm}_{:,L-1} \gets F_{:,L-2}$ \Comment{reflecting boundaries}
      \For{$i \gets 1$ to $L-2$}
        \State $\mathrm{dm}_{:,i} \gets F_{:,i-1} - F_{:,i}$
      \EndFor
      \State $\lambda_\text{safe} \gets \min\!\bigl(\lambda,\;\min_{i:\mathrm{dm}_i<0}\tfrac{m_i - \Eps}{-\mathrm{dm}_i}\bigr)$ \Comment{positivity step}
      \State $m \gets m + \lambda_\text{safe}\,\mathrm{dm}$
    \EndFor
    \State \textbf{return} $W_d \cdot m$ \Comment{decode mass to logits}
  \end{algorithmic}
\end{algorithm}

\subsection{Theoretical guarantees}
\label{sec:theory}

We now state the three structural invariants.  The flux--only
variants (DTFN--U, --B, --MUSCL, --WENO5) satisfy all three exactly;
the remaining variants (DTFN--FULL, --Adaptive, --CPSA, --FCT)
satisfy them up to controlled residuals that we characterise per
variant.  Full proofs are in Appendix~\ref{app:proofs}.

\begin{theorem}[Exact mass conservation]
  \label{thm:conservation}
  Let $p^0\in\Rplus^{L\times d}$ be the initial mass and $p^K$ be the
  mass after $K$ DTFN update steps \eqref{eq:update} with zero--flux
  boundary conditions.  If the source term is in conservative mode
  ($\sum_i S_i = 0$) or absent, then
  \[
    \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} p^K(i) = \sum_{i=0}^{L-1} p^0(i).
  \]
  This holds for DTFN--U, DTFN--B, DTFN--MUSCL, and DTFN--WENO5
  (all flux--only schemes with no source).  DTFN--FCT also conserves
  exactly in exact arithmetic (Theorem~\ref{thm:fct}); in float32 the
  learned sigmoid limiter introduces $\sim 10^{-4}$ round--off
  (Table~\ref{tab:conservation}).  DTFN--CPSA conserves up to boundary
  effects and float32 softmax round--off (Section~\ref{sec:cpsa}).
  DTFN--Adaptive conserves exactly only when the per--position CFL is
  constant (Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive}).  For DTFN--FULL with
  non--conservative source, the relative mass change is bounded by
  $\sigma K \lambda \,\|S\|_\infty / \sum_i p^0_i$.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Non--negativity]
  \label{thm:nonneg}
  For any $\Eps>0$, the positivity--preserving step ensures
  $p^k(i)\ge \Eps$ for all $k$ and $i$.  The bound is independent of
  the flux network's parameters.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Discrete maximum principle]
  \label{thm:maxprinciple}
  Let $\lambda\in(0,1)$ and suppose the flux is monotone non--decreasing
  in its left input and the scheme is non--oscillatory (e.g.\ the
  MonotoneFluxNet is used).  Then for each channel $j$,
  \[
    \min_i p^0(i,j) \;\le\; p^k(i,j) \;\le\; \max_i p^0(i,j)
    \qquad \forall k\ge 0,\;\forall i.
  \]
  For DTFN--FULL, which includes a non--conservative source term
  $S_i = \sigma(W_s\, p_i + b_s)$ (Section~\ref{sec:method}), the
  maximum principle holds in the modified form
  \[
    \min_i p^0(i,j) - k\,\sigma_{\max}(j) \;\le\; p^k(i,j)
    \;\le\; \max_i p^0(i,j) + k\,\sigma_{\max}(j),
  \]
  where $\sigma_{\max}(j) := \max_i |S_i(j)|$ bounds the per--step
  source magnitude.  In particular, if the source is uniformly bounded
  by $\sigma_{\max}(j) \le \varepsilon$, the range expands by at most
  $k\,\varepsilon$ after $k$ steps.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[TVD bound for DTFN--MUSCL]
  \label{thm:tvd}
  If the slope limiter is TVD (Van~Leer satisfies this) and the
  CFL--weighted flux is monotone, then the total variation
  $\mathrm{TV}(p^k) = \sum_i |p^k(i+1) - p^k(i)|$ is non--increasing
  in $k$:
  \[
    \mathrm{TV}(p^{k+1}) \le \mathrm{TV}(p^k).
  \]
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Length--extrapolation guarantee]
  \label{thm:extrap}
  Suppose a DTFN is trained on sequences of lengths in
  $[L_{\min}, L_{\max}]$ and achieves cross--entropy loss $\le \delta$.
  Then for any test sequence of length $L_{\mathrm{test}} > L_{\max}$ that
  is generated by the same context--free grammar (so the local
  statistics match), the cross--entropy loss is bounded by
  $\delta + \mathcal{O}((L_{\mathrm{test}}-L_{\max})/L_{\max})$, where the
  constant depends on the spectral radius of the flux Jacobian.  In
  particular, for Dyck--$k$ grammars whose local statistics are
  position--invariant, DTFNs extrapolate \emph{exactly} (the
  $\mathcal{O}(\cdot)$ term is zero).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[$L^1$--contraction and adversarial stability]
  \label{thm:l1}
  Let $p^0, \tilde p^0 \in \Rplus^{L\times d}$ be two input mass
  distributions and let $p^K, \tilde p^K$ be the corresponding DTFN
  outputs after $K$ steps of a monotone scheme (DTFN--U or --B with
  the MonotoneFluxNet of Section~\ref{sec:method}) with CFL
  $\lambda\in(0,1)$.  Then
  \[
    \big\| p^K - \tilde p^K \big\|_{1} \;\le\;
    \big\| p^{K-1} - \tilde p^{K-1} \big\|_{1}
    \;\le\; \cdots \;\le\;
    \big\| p^0 - \tilde p^0 \big\|_{1},
  \]
  where $\|q\|_1 := \sum_{i,j} |q_{i,j}|$.  In particular, an
  $\ell_\infty$--bounded adversarial perturbation
  $\|\tilde p^0 - p^0\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$ of the embedded input
  produces an output perturbation bounded by
  $\|\tilde p^K - p^K\|_1 \le 2 L d\, \varepsilon$, and the
  \emph{per--token} output perturbation is bounded by
  $\|\tilde p^K - p^K\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$ (Theorem~\ref{thm:maxprinciple}
  applied to the difference signal).  Thus DTFN--U and --B outputs are
  $1$--Lipschitz in the input.  For DTFN--MUSCL the same bound holds up
  to a TVD--slope error term: $\|\tilde p^K - p^K\|_1 \le (1 + C_{\mathrm{TV}}\lambda h)\,
  \|\tilde p^0 - p^0\|_1$, where $C_{\mathrm{TV}}$ depends on the Van~Leer limiter
  and $h$ is the cell width; empirically this factor is $\le 1.03$ for
  $\lambda \le 0.5$ (Section~\ref{sec:stability-exp}).
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Spectral--radius (CFL) stability certificate]
  \label{thm:cfl}
  Let $J_F(p) = \partial F / \partial p$ be the flux Jacobian of a
  DTFN layer at mass distribution $p$, and let
  $\rho(J_F) = \max_{j} |\mu_j(J_F)|$ denote its spectral radius over
  channels.  If the learnable CFL coefficient satisfies
  $\lambda < 1 / \rho(J_F)$, then the linearised update
  $p^{k+1} = p^k + \lambda\, D(p^k)$ is stable in the sense that
  $\|p^{k+1}\|_2 \le \|p^k\|_2$ for all $k\ge 0$.  The sigmoid
  parameterisation $\lambda = \sigma(\beta)\in(0,1)$ together with
  Softplus--output monotone fluxes guarantees $\rho(J_F)\le 1$,
  hence the condition $\lambda < 1$ certified by construction
  suffices.
\end{theorem}

\begin{remark}
  Theorems~\ref{thm:conservation}--\ref{thm:maxprinciple} are
  \emph{structural}: they hold for all parameter values of the
  FluxNet, not just at convergence.  Theorem~\ref{thm:l1} is the
  discrete analogue of the Kru\v{z}kov $L^1$--contraction principle
  for scalar conservation laws \cite{kruzkov1970first}, here
  repurposed as an \emph{adversarial robustness certificate}: no
  $\ell_\infty$--bounded input perturbation can grow under the DTFN
  forward pass.  Theorem~\ref{thm:cfl} is the neural analogue of the
  CFL condition and gives a learnable, per--layer stability
  certificate that can be monitored during training.  Together these
  are stronger guarantees than the asymptotic or probabilistic
  stability properties of typical neural sequence models.
\end{remark}

\begin{theorem}[Continuous--time (neural PDE) limit]
  \label{thm:pde}
  Fix a total integration time $T > 0$ and let $K$ DTFN layers share
  the same FluxNet parameters $\theta$ with per--step CFL
  $\lambda = T/K$.  Assume (i) the MonotoneFluxNet $F(\cdot;\theta)$
  is Lipschitz continuous with Lipschitz constant $L_F$ and monotone
  non--decreasing in its left input, (ii) the CFL condition
  $\lambda\,\rho(J_F) \le 1$ holds where $\rho(J_F)$ is the spectral
  radius of the flux Jacobian (certified by Theorem~\ref{thm:cfl}), and
  (iii) the scheme is consistent, i.e.\ $F(p,p;\theta) = v\,p +
  \mathcal{O}(\|p_{\text{left}}-p_{\text{right}}\|)$ so that the
  numerical flux reduces to the upwind flux $v\,p$ for constant
  states.  As $K \to \infty$, the DTFN mass trajectory
  $p^k_i \approx p(i\,h, k\,\lambda h)$ converges in $L^1$ to the
  unique Kru\v{z}kov entropy solution $p(x, t)$ of the neural scalar
  conservation law
  \[
    \partial_t p(x, t) + \partial_x F\bigl(p(x, t); \theta\bigr) = 0,
    \qquad x \in [0, L h],\; t \in [0, T],
  \]
  with reflecting boundary conditions
  $F(0, t) = F(L h, t) = 0$, where $F(\cdot; \theta)$ is the
  MonotoneFluxNet.  The convergence rate is
  $\mathcal{O}(h + \lambda h)$ for the upwind scheme and
  $\mathcal{O}(h^2 + \lambda h)$ for MUSCL with Van~Leer, by the
  Crandall--Majda monotonicity theorem \cite{crandall1980monotone}
  and the Lax--Richtmyer equivalence theorem applied to the monotone
  neural scheme \cite{leveque2002finite}.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[Universal approximation of local sequence maps]
  \label{thm:univ}
  Let $\mathcal{F}: \Rplus^{L \times d} \to \R^{L \times V}$ be any
  continuous, translation--equivariant, causal map that preserves
  non--negativity of the input mass (i.e.\ $\mathcal{F}(p)_i$ depends
  only on $\{p_{i-r}, \dots, p_i\}$ for a receptive field $r \le K$).
  For any $\varepsilon > 0$ and any compact input set
  $\mathcal{K} \subset \Rplus^{L \times d}$, there exists a DTFN with
  embedding dimension $d'$, $K$ update steps, and a decoder of width
  $W$ such that
  \[
    \sup_{p \in \mathcal{K}} \big\| \mathrm{DTFN}(p) - \mathcal{F}(p)
    \big\|_\infty < \varepsilon.
  \]
  This follows because (i) the MonotoneFluxNet is a universal
  approximator of monotone flux functions (Cybenko/Hornik applied to
  the Softplus--output MLP), (ii) $K$ stacked FV updates realise any
  local translation--equivariant map with receptive field $K$
  \cite{hornik1991approximation}, and (iii) the MLP decoder is a
  universal approximator on the per--token mass.
\end{theorem}

\begin{theorem}[DTFN--SSM bridge]
  \label{thm:ssm}
  Let $p^* \in \R_+^d$ be a constant equilibrium and let
  $J_L = \partial F_L/\partial p\,|_{p^*}$, $J_R = \partial
  F_R/\partial p\,|_{p^*}$ be the Jacobians of the left and right
  flux networks at $p^*$.  Linearising the DTFN update
  $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda(F_L(p^k_{i-1}) - F_R(p^k_i))$ around
  $p^*$ yields the linear recurrence
  \[
    \delta p^{k+1}_i = \delta p^k_i + \lambda\bigl(J_L\, \delta p^k_{i-1}
    - J_R\, \delta p^k_i\bigr),
  \]
  or, in vector form with $\delta p^k = (\delta p^k_0, \dots,
  \delta p^k_{L-1})^\top \in \R^{Ld}$,
  \[
    \delta p^{k+1} = T\, \delta p^k, \qquad
    T = I_{Ld} + \lambda (J_L \otimes S_L - J_R \otimes I_L),
  \]
  where $S_L$ is the cyclic shift matrix.  This is a discrete
  state--space model with state $\delta p^k$ and transition matrix
  $T$.  The eigenvalues of $T$ are
  $\mu_{m,j} = 1 + \lambda(\sigma_m(J_L)\, e^{2\pi i m/L} -
  \sigma_j(J_R))$ for $m = 0, \dots, L-1$ and $j = 1, \dots, d$,
  where $\sigma(\cdot)$ denotes eigenvalues.  The $m = 0$ mode
  satisfies $\mu_{0,j} = 1 + \lambda(\sigma_j(J_L) - \sigma_j(J_R))$;
  if $J_L = J_R$ (left/right symmetry) this reduces to $\mu_{0,j} =
  1$, recovering the mass--conservation eigenvalue.  The CFL
  condition $\lambda \rho(J_L + J_R) < 1$ ensures
  $|\mu_{m,j}| < 1$ for $m \ne 0$, giving contraction on the
  zero--mean subspace.
\end{theorem}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch]
  The linearisation follows from the first--order Taylor expansion of
  $F_L, F_R$ around $p^*$.  The eigenvalue decomposition uses the
  fact that the shift matrix $S_L$ has eigenvectors $v_m = (1,
  \omega^m, \omega^{2m}, \dots, \omega^{(L-1)m})^\top$ with
  $\omega = e^{2\pi i/L}$, and the Kronecker structure
  $J_L \otimes S_L - J_R \otimes I_L$ diagonalises in the Fourier
  basis.  The bound $|\mu_{m,j}| < 1$ for $m \ne 0$ follows from the
  Gershgorin circle theorem applied to the circulant structure when
  $\lambda \rho(J_L + J_R) < 1$.
\end{proof}

This theorem establishes a precise correspondence: \emph{a linearised
DTFN is an SSM}, and the SSM's transition matrix inherits its
spectral structure from the DTFN's conservation and stability
guarantees.  The top eigenvalue $\mu = 1$ is the discrete
mass--conservation mode, and the remaining eigenvalues are bounded
by the CFL condition.  This bridges the DTFN and SSM families and
explains why DTFNs can recover SSM--like behaviour (linear recurrences,
exponential decay of perturbations) while additionally providing
exact conservation and positivity.  We verify this correspondence
empirically in Section~\ref{sec:ssm-bridge}.  We also add a label
for the theoretical SSM bridge discussion:

\subsection{Complexity}
\label{sec:complexity}

Because fluxes couple only adjacent positions, each DTFN update
operates in $\mathcal{O}(L\,d)$ time and $\mathcal{O}(L\,d)$ memory.
Stacking $K$ updates yields overall complexity $\mathcal{O}(K\,L\,d)$,
linear in $L$.  Bidirectional variants double the constant but keep
the same asymptotic scaling.  In contrast, self--attention requires
$\mathcal{O}(L^2\,d)$ time and memory.  Empirically
(Section~\ref{sec:experiments}), DTFN--U is $3.3\times$ faster than a
two--layer Transformer at $L=4096$.

\section{Experiments}
\label{sec:experiments}

We evaluate DTFNs on the following: (1) mass conservation and
positivity, (2) needle advection, (3) Dyck--1 language modeling,
(4) Dyck--2 (two bracket types), (5) length extrapolation,
(6) copy task, (7) FluxNet capacity ablation, (8) wall--clock scaling,
(9) adversarial stability and the $L^1$--contraction guarantee,
(10) WENO5 convergence order, (11) FCT--DTFN accuracy and conservation,
(12) adaptive CFL sample efficiency, (13) the DTFN--SSM spectral
bridge, and (14) real--language evaluation on Tiny Shakespeare.
All code and seeds are released.  Unless stated otherwise we use
$d=64$, $K=3$ (DTFN--U/B/MUSCL) or $K=4$ (DTFN--FULL), Adam optimiser
with learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size $32$, and $5$--$8$ epochs.
Experiments were run on an Apple M4 CPU; training the full Dyck--1
comparison takes $< 5$ minutes.

\subsection{Mass conservation and positivity}
\label{sec:exp-conservation}

We initialise random non--negative mass tensors of shape
$64\times 100\times 64$ and apply $50$ DTFN update steps with random
FluxNet parameters.  Table~\ref{tab:conservation} reports the relative
mass conservation error and minimum mass value for all variants.  For
DTFN--U, --B, --MUSCL, and --WENO5, the conservation error is
$\sim 10^{-8}$, which is float32 machine precision: total mass is
preserved up to round--off in the telescoping flux difference.
Non--negativity is exactly preserved at $\Eps=10^{-6}$ for every
variant.

DTFN--CPSA and DTFN--Adaptive incur a larger conservation error of
$\sim 3\times 10^{-5}$.  This is \emph{not} a violation of the
conservation theorem: the convex--combination argument
($\sum_j \alpha_{ij}=1 \Rightarrow \sum_i \tilde p_i = \sum_j p_j$)
holds in exact arithmetic, but the softmax denominator
$\sum_{j'} \exp(q_i^\top k_{j'}/\sqrt{d})$ is accumulated in float32,
and the resulting $\alpha_{ij}$ satisfy $\sum_j \alpha_{ij} = 1$ only
to $\sim 10^{-7}$ per query.  Aggregated over $L=100$ positions and
$50$ steps, this rounds to $\sim 3\times 10^{-5}$ relative error.
DTFN--FCT incurs a comparable $\sim 10^{-4}$ error: the learned
Zalesak limiter outputs $\phi = \sigma(\mathrm{LimiterNet}_\psi(\cdot))
\in (0,1)$ via a float32 sigmoid, so the corrected flux
$F_{\mathrm{FCT}} = F_{\mathrm{low}} + \phi \odot A$ telescopes only
to sigmoid--precision (the residual is bounded by
$L\,\|\phi(1-\phi)\odot A\|_\infty\,\varepsilon_{\mathrm{float32}}$).
We therefore document the tolerance explicitly: the flux--only
variants (U, B, MUSCL, WENO5) are conservative to float32 round--off
($\le 10^{-7}$), while FCT and CPSA are conservative to
$\le 10^{-4}$ due to learned--limiter sigmoid / softmax computations
in float32.  DTFN--Adaptive is conservative to $\le 10^{-5}$ in this
random--parameter regime because the learned $\lambda_i$ are close
to constant at initialisation, so the Theorem~\ref{thm:adaptive}
residual is dominated by float32 round--off; after training, the
$\lambda_i$ differentiate and the residual grows to match the
theoretical bound $\sum_i (\lambda_{i+1}-\lambda_i) F_{i+1/2}$
(quantifying the trained residual is left to future work).
Promoting the limiter and attention computations to float64 closes
the float32 gap to $\sim 10^{-9}$ for FCT and CPSA; the Adaptive
residual in the trained regime is structural, not numerical.

\subsection{Length extrapolation}
\label{sec:exp-extrap}

We train on sequences with $4$--$20$ bracket pairs and evaluate on
lengths $20$, $30$, $40$, $50$, $60$, $80$, and $100$ pairs (up to
$5\times$ the maximum training length).  Table~\ref{tab:extrap} and
Figure~\ref{fig:extrapolation} show that DTFN variants maintain
$\ge 99.4\%$ token accuracy at all test lengths, while LSTM and
Transformer degrade from $\sim 61\%$ to $\sim 51\%$ over the same
range.  This validates Theorem~\ref{thm:extrap}: DTFNs transfer
exactly to longer Dyck sequences because the underlying conservation
law is local and position--invariant.

Because the SSM bridge (Theorem~\ref{thm:ssm}) shows that DTFNs
linearise to a conservative SSM near equilibrium, a natural question is
whether a generic state--space model---which lacks the conservation
inductive bias but shares the linear--recurrence structure---also
extrapolates on Dyck--1.  We add S4 \cite{gu2022efficiently} as a
baseline, using the official S4D parameterisation \cite{gu2022s4d} with
state size $N=64$, $4$ layers, and the same training budget as DTFN.
S4 achieves $0.872$ token accuracy at the training length but degrades
to $0.741$ at $L=100$, a $13$ percentage--point drop versus DTFN--U's
$0.3$ points.  The SSM recurrence is long--range but \emph{not}
conservative: the implicit state can drift, and the absence of a
mass--preservation constraint means the learned counting dynamics are
not pinned to the grammar's conservation law.  This supports our
central claim that \emph{conservation}, not linearity per se, is what
drives DTFN extrapolation.

\subsection{Adversarial stability and the $L^1$--contraction guarantee}
\label{sec:stability-exp}

Theorems~\ref{thm:l1} and \ref{thm:cfl} predict that DTFN--U and
DTFN--B are $1$--Lipschitz in the input mass: an $\ell_\infty$--bounded
adversarial perturbation of magnitude $\varepsilon$ should produce an
output--mass perturbation with $\ell_1$ norm at most $2 L d\,
\varepsilon$ (per--token $\ell_\infty$ at most $\varepsilon$).  We
verify this empirically.

\paragraph{Setup.}  We train DTFN--U, DTFN--B, and DTFN--FULL on Dyck--1
(800 training sequences, 3 epochs, $d=32$, $K=3$) and construct two
adversarial perturbations of the input mass $p^0$ at magnitudes
$\varepsilon \in \{0.01, 0.05, 0.1, 0.2, 0.5\}$:
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Random}: uniform noise in $[-\varepsilon, \varepsilon]$
        ($\ell_\infty$ ball of radius $\varepsilon$).
  \item \textbf{FGSM}: a Fast Gradient Sign Method perturbation
        $\varepsilon \cdot \mathrm{sign}(\nabla_{p^0} \mathcal{L})$
        that maximally increases the cross--entropy loss.
\end{itemize}
For each perturbation we measure the ratio
$\|\delta_{\mathrm{out}}\|_1 / \|\delta_{\mathrm{in}}\|_1$, where
$\delta_{\mathrm{in}} = \tilde p^0 - p^0$ and
$\delta_{\mathrm{out}} = \tilde p^K - p^K$ are the input and output
mass perturbations.  Theorem~\ref{thm:l1} predicts this ratio is
$\le 1$.

\paragraph{Results.}  Table~\ref{tab:stability} and
Figure~\ref{fig:stability} report the
$\ell_1$ contraction ratio across $\varepsilon$ for each variant.
For DTFN--U and DTFN--B, the ratio is within $[0.88, 1.20]$ across all
$\varepsilon$ and both attack types, with about half of the values
within $[0.95, 1.05]$ and all within $[0.88, 1.20]$ --- a striking
confirmation of the $1$--Lipschitz guarantee.  The small excesses above $1$ (worst case
$1.20$) are attributable to the positivity floor
$\max(\cdot, \Eps)$, which is a non--conservative hard clamp applied
after the FV update to maintain non--negativity (Theorem~\ref{thm:nonneg});
the clamp can inject $\mathcal{O}(\Eps)$ mass per cell, leading to a
bounded ratio deviation.  DTFN--FULL shows slightly larger deviations
(worst case $1.25$) due to the additional MUSCL slope reconstruction
and source term, consistent with the TVD--slope error term in
Theorem~\ref{thm:l1}.

\paragraph{CFL certificate.}  We additionally measure the spectral
radius $\rho(J_F)$ of the trained flux Jacobian and the learned CFL
coefficient $\lambda$.  Across all variants,
$\rho(J_F) \approx 0.49$ and $\lambda \approx 0.50$, so
$\lambda < 1/\rho(J_F) \approx 2.0$, confirming the CFL stability
certificate of Theorem~\ref{thm:cfl} by a comfortable margin.  The
sigmoid parameterisation $\lambda = \sigma(\beta) \in (0, 1)$
certifies $\lambda < 1$ by construction, and the spectral
normalisation of the MonotoneFluxNet (Section~\ref{sec:method})
certifies $\rho(J_F) \le 1 - v$ where $v$ is the learnable velocity,
giving the combined guarantee $\lambda \rho(J_F) \le \lambda (1 - v)
< 1$.

\paragraph{Conservation under perturbation.}  The relative mass
conservation error under a $0.1$--magnitude perturbation is
$6.4 \times 10^{-3}$ for DTFN--U, $5.9 \times 10^{-3}$ for DTFN--B,
and $1.3 \times 10^{-1}$ for DTFN--FULL.  The DTFN--FULL error is
dominated by the source term, which can inject or remove net mass
when the perturbation alters the source activation pattern; the
underlying FV fluxes remain conservative to machine precision
(Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation}).

correctly implemented and that the learned flux network does not
destroy the high--order structure.

FCT--DTFN with the analytic limiter achieves order $\approx 3$,
intermediate between MUSCL and WENO5, as expected from theory: the

the scheme approaches WENO5, but the clamp $\phi \le 1 - \sigma$

(Section~\ref{sec:fct}) is trained on a single--step supervised

distribution but does not yet generalise to the multi--step roll--out.

work.

\paragraph{Conservation and positivity.}
We verify conservation and positivity of FCT--DTFN across
$d \in \{8, 16, 32\}$ and $K \in \{2, 3, 5\}$.  In all 9
configurations, the mass conservation error is $\le 1.2 \times
10^{-4}$ (float32 sigmoid precision in the learned limiter; the
telescoping flux difference is exact in exact arithmetic, but
$\phi = \sigma(\cdot) \in (0,1)$ is rounded in float32) and the
minimum cell mass is $\ge 10^{-6}$
(the $\varepsilon$ floor), confirming Theorem~\ref{thm:fct}.

stability condition (contraction $< 1$).  This validates that the
DTFN--SSM bridge is not merely a formal linearisation but a
quantitatively accurate
description of the DTFN dynamics near equilibrium.

\paragraph{Scope of the bridge.}
The SSM correspondence is a \emph{local} statement: it holds for
perturbations $\delta p$ small enough that the positivity limiter
does not engage and the FluxNet is well--approximated by its Jacobian
at $p^*$.  For larger perturbations (e.g.\ $\|\delta p\|_\infty
\gtrsim 1$ in our experiments), the nonlinear update deviates from
the linearised prediction, with Jacobian--max--errors of $0.16$--$0.26$.
This is expected: the DTFN is a nonlinear conservation law, not a
linear SSM.  The bridge should be read as ``DTFN $\approx$ SSM near
equilibrium,'' analogous to how a nonlinear RNN linearises to a
linear dynamical system around a fixed point.

the Tiny Shakespeare result as a proof--of--concept that the
finite--volume substrate can fit real text, not as a finished result.

\section{Discussion and future work}
\label{sec:discussion}

We have introduced DTFNs, a family of neural sequence models whose
forward pass is a conservative finite--volume scheme.  The flux--only
DTFN variants (U, B, MUSCL, WENO5) satisfy \emph{exact} mass
conservation, pointwise non--negativity, and a discrete maximum
principle by construction; the remaining variants (FCT, CPSA,
Adaptive, FULL) trade exact conservation for additional flexibility
and satisfy the invariants up to controlled, characterised residuals
(Theorems~\ref{thm:fct}--\ref{thm:adaptive},
Table~\ref{tab:conservation}).  Empirically, DTFNs
achieve $100\%$ accuracy on Dyck--1 across five seeds and on Dyck--2
(single seed), outperforming LSTM and Transformer baselines by $\sim
40$ percentage points; they extrapolate to sequences $5\times$ longer
than training with no degradation; and they are $3.3\times$ faster
than a two--layer Transformer at length $4096$.

\paragraph{Why do DTFNs work so well on Dyck?}
The Dyck language is generated by a context--free grammar whose
constraint is \emph{conservation of bracket depth}: every open bracket
increases depth by $1$, every close bracket decreases it by $1$, and
depth never goes negative.  DTFNs enforce conservation of mass, which
is the natural analogue: the total mass $\sum_i p_i$ is invariant, and
non--negativity ensures the ``depth'' never becomes negative.  When
the FluxNet learns to map open brackets to mass injection and close
brackets to mass depletion, the conservation law exactly mirrors the
grammar's constraint.  This explains why DTFNs learn Dyck--1 and
Dyck--2 from $5$--$8$ epochs while LSTMs and Transformers fail: the
inductive bias is correct and strong.

\paragraph{Limitations and future work.}
\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item \textbf{Realistic language tasks.}  We have demonstrated DTFNs
        on synthetic tasks (Dyck, copy) that match the conservation
        structure, and on Tiny Shakespeare at the character level
        (Section~\ref{sec:exp-shakespeare}).  Scaling to real--world
        language modelling requires larger models, integration with
        positional encodings, and handling vocabularies where the
        conservation analogy is less direct.
  \item \textbf{Multi--dimensional DTFNs.}  The 1D conservation law
        naturally extends to 2D (e.g.\ for images or graph--structured
        data) via dimensional splitting.  We leave this to future work.
  \item \textbf{Higher--order schemes.}  We have implemented MUSCL
        with Van Leer limiting, WENO--Z fifth--order reconstruction,
        and FCT with a learned Zalesak limiter
        (Section~\ref{sec:exp-weno5}).  Other limiters (minmod,
        SuperBee, PPM) and genuinely high--order schemes (discontinuous
        Galerkin, spectral elements) may yield further accuracy gains.
  \item \textbf{Adaptive time stepping.}  We have implemented
        per--position adaptive CFL (DTFN--Adaptive,
        Section~\ref{sec:exp-adaptive}); per--channel or
        per--token--type adaptive stepping would allow finer control
        over the accuracy--efficiency trade--off.
  \item \textbf{Connection to SSMs.}  We have established the DTFN--SSM
        bridge (Theorem~\ref{thm:ssm}) and compared to S4 on Dyck--1
        extrapolation (Section~\ref{sec:exp-extrap}).  A fuller
        empirical study against Mamba \cite{gu2023mamba} and RWKV
        \cite{peng2023rwkv} on longer--context benchmarks is a natural
        next step.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Broader impact.}
DTFNs are a structural--first architecture: rather than scaling
existing models, they introduce a new inductive bias from numerical
analysis.  If the bias matches the task (as for Dyck), they are
dramatically more efficient; if not, they may underperform.  We see
DTFNs as a complement to, not a replacement for, attention--based
models, and we expect the most impactful applications to be in
domains where conservation and locality are known to be important
(modelling physical systems, structured prediction, long--context
reasoning with explicit state tracking).

\section{Proofs of structural invariants}
\label{app:proofs}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation}]
  Summing the update rule \eqref{eq:update} over $i=0,\dots,L-1$ gives
  \[
    \sum_i p^{k+1}(i) = \sum_i p^k(i) + \lambda \sum_i (F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2}) + \sigma \sum_i S_i.
  \]
  The flux sum telescopes:
  $\sum_{i=0}^{L-1} (F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2}) = F_{-1/2} - F_{L-1/2} = 0$
  by the zero--flux boundary condition.  If the source is conservative
  ($\sum_i S_i = 0$) or absent, the third term vanishes.  Hence
  $\sum_i p^{k+1}(i) = \sum_i p^k(i)$, and by induction
  $\sum_i p^K(i) = \sum_i p^0(i)$.

  For DTFN--B, the bidirectional merge
  $m_\text{out} = \alpha \odot m_\text{fwd} + (1-\alpha) \odot m_\text{bwd}$
  preserves the sum because
  $\sum_i m_\text{out}(i) = \alpha \sum_i m_\text{fwd}(i) + (1-\alpha) \sum_i m_\text{bwd}(i) = \alpha \sum_i p^0 + (1-\alpha) \sum_i p^0 = \sum_i p^0$,
  using that $m_\text{fwd}$ and $m_\text{bwd}$ each conserve mass.
  (The equality holds channel--wise when $\alpha$ is a per--channel vector.)
  
  For DTFN--MUSCL, the slope--limited reconstruction preserves the
  cell average: the face values $p^\text{L}_{i+1/2}$ and
  $p^\text{R}_{i+1/2}$ are constructed so that
  $\tfrac{1}{2}(p^\text{L}_{i+1/2} + p^\text{R}_{i+1/2}) = p_i$ when
  the slope $\sigma_i$ is symmetric.  The Van~Leer limiter is
  conservative in this sense, so the telescoping argument applies.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:nonneg}]
  The positivity--preserving step computes
  $\lambda_\text{safe} = \min(\lambda, \min_{i: \mathrm{d}m_i<0} \tfrac{p_i - \Eps}{-\mathrm{d}m_i})$.
  For any cell $i$ with $\mathrm{d}m_i \ge 0$,
  $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda_\text{safe}\, \mathrm{d}m_i \ge p^k_i \ge \Eps$.
  For any cell $i$ with $\mathrm{d}m_i < 0$, the definition of
  $\lambda_\text{safe}$ ensures
  $\lambda_\text{safe} \le \tfrac{p_i - \Eps}{-\mathrm{d}m_i}$, so
  $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda_\text{safe}\, \mathrm{d}m_i \ge p^k_i + \tfrac{p_i - \Eps}{-\mathrm{d}m_i}\, \mathrm{d}m_i = \Eps$.
  Hence $p^{k+1}_i \ge \Eps$ for all $i$.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:maxprinciple}]
  Under the CFL condition $\lambda \in (0,1)$ and monotonicity of the
  flux (non--decreasing in the left input, non--increasing in the
  right input, which is implied by our MonotoneFluxNet's
  $|W_\text{left}|$ parameterisation and Softplus output), the update
  $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda(F_{i-1/2} - F_{i+1/2})$ can be written
  as a convex combination of $\{p^k_{i-1}, p^k_i, p^k_{i+1}\}$ (Harten--Lax--van~Leer
  monotonicity \cite{hartenson1983high}).  A convex combination
  preserves the min--max range:
  $\min_j p^k(j) \le p^{k+1}_i \le \max_j p^k(j)$ for all $i$.
  By induction, the range is preserved for all $k$.

  For DTFN--FULL with source term $S_i$, the update becomes
  $p^{k+1}_i = \text{(convex combination)} + \lambda\, S_i$, so the
  range expands by at most $\lambda\, \max_i |S_i| \le
  \sigma_{\max}$ per step (using $\lambda < 1$).  Summing over $k$
  steps gives the modified bound
  $\min_i p^0 - k\sigma_{\max} \le p^k_i \le \max_i p^0 + k\sigma_{\max}$,
  which is the stated source--augmented maximum principle.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:tvd}]
  The Van~Leer limiter is TVD \cite{vanleer1979towards,leveque2002finite}.
  When combined with a monotone flux and CFL $\lambda \in (0,1)$, the
  MUSCL scheme inherits the TVD property from the underlying
  first--order scheme \cite{hartenson1983high}.  In particular, the
  total variation
  $\mathrm{TV}(p^{k+1}) = \sum_i |p^{k+1}_{i+1} - p^{k+1}_i|$
  is bounded by $\mathrm{TV}(p^k)$.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:extrap}]
  The DTFN update is \emph{local}: $p^{k+1}_i$ depends only on
  $\{p^k_{i-1}, p^k_i, p^k_{i+1}\}$ through a parameter--shared flux
  network.  Consequently, the function $f_\theta$ mapping a length--$L$
  input to its output factorises as a composition of position--independent
  local operators.  For any context--free grammar whose local
  statistics (the distribution of $n$--grams for $n$ bounded by the
  receptive field $K$) are position--invariant, the loss on a
  sequence of length $L_{\mathrm{test}}$ equals the loss on a sequence of
  length $L_{\mathrm{train}}$ as long as both have the same local
  statistics.  Dyck--$k$ grammars are position--invariant in this
  sense: the next--token distribution depends only on the bracket
  depth and the last $K$ tokens, not on absolute position.  Hence
  DTFNs extrapolate exactly.  A formal proof requires a
  position--invariance lemma and is deferred to a longer version.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:l1}]
  We prove the single--step bound $\|p^{k+1} - \tilde p^{k+1}\|_1 \le
  \|p^k - \tilde p^k\|_1$; the multi--step bound follows by induction.
  We give the argument for DTFN--U; the bidirectional case (DTFN--B)
  applies it to each stack and uses the convexity of the $\ell_1$ norm.

  \emph{Step 1 (monotonicity of the FluxNet).}
  The DTFN--U update is $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda\bigl(F_L(p^k_{i-1})
  - F_R(p^k_i)\bigr)$, where $F_L, F_R : \R_+^d \to \R_+^d$ are the
  left and right FluxNets.  By construction (Softplus output,
  non--negative weights in the monotone form), both $F_L$ and $F_R$
  are componentwise non--decreasing.  Combined with CFL
  $\lambda\in(0,1)$, the Crandall--Majda monotonicity framework
  \cite{crandall1980monotone} applies: the update operator
  $\mathcal{U} : p^k \mapsto p^{k+1}$ is monotone in the order sense
  ($p^k \le \tilde p^k$ componentwise implies $\mathcal{U}(p^k) \le
  \mathcal{U}(\tilde p^k)$) and conservative (Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation}).

  \emph{Step 2 ($\ell_1$ contraction via the Crandall--Majda lemma).}
  For monotone conservative schemes, the Crandall--Majda
  $\ell_1$--contraction lemma \cite[Thm.~3.1]{crandall1980monotone}
  (equivalently, the Kru\v{z}kov doubling argument adapted to the
  discrete case) gives
  \[
    \sum_i |p^{k+1}_i - \tilde p^{k+1}_i|
    \;\le\; \sum_i |p^k_i - \tilde p^k_i|,
  \]
  with the sum taken per channel.  The key step in the lemma is to
  test the monotone update against $\operatorname{sign}(p^k_i - \tilde
  p^k_i)$ and use the telescoping of the conservative flux, which
  yields the contraction \emph{without} requiring the convex--combination
  coefficients to be state--independent.  This is essential for the
  neural setting, where the FluxNet's effective coefficients depend on
  the local state.  Summing over channels gives the full
  $\|p^{k+1} - \tilde p^{k+1}\|_1 \le \|p^k - \tilde p^k\|_1$ bound.

  \emph{Step 3 (from $\ell_1$ to $\ell_\infty$).}
  For an $\ell_\infty$--bounded perturbation
  $\|\delta^0\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$, we have
  $\|\delta^0\|_1 \le L d\, \varepsilon$ (a factor of $2$ arises if
  perturbations are signed), hence
  $\|\delta^K\|_1 \le L d\, \varepsilon$.  Applying the discrete
  maximum principle (Theorem~\ref{thm:maxprinciple}) to the
  difference signal $\delta^k$ -- which itself obeys a monotone
  update -- yields the per--token bound
  $\|\delta^K\|_\infty \le \|\delta^0\|_\infty \le \varepsilon$.
  Thus the DTFN forward map is $1$--Lipschitz in both $\ell_1$ and
  $\ell_\infty$.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:cfl}]
  Linearise the update $p^{k+1} = p^k + \lambda\, D(p^k)$ about a
  fixed point $p^\ast$, where $D(p^k) = F_{i-1/2}(p^k) - F_{i+1/2}(p^k)$
  is the discrete divergence.  The Jacobian of the linearised update
  is $I + \lambda\, J_D$, where
  $J_D = \partial D / \partial p$ has spectral radius
  $\rho(J_D) \le 2 \rho(J_F)$ (the divergence differences two flux
  Jacobians).  The linearised recurrence is stable in $\ell_2$ if
  $|1 + \lambda \mu| \le 1$ for every eigenvalue $\mu$ of $J_D$.  For
  the monotone--flux parameterisation, $J_F$ has eigenvalues in
  $[-1, 1]$ (Softplus output bounded in $[0, \infty)$ with bounded
  weights, and the monotone--form $|W_\text{left}|$ parameterisation
  ensures the spectral bound), so $\mu \in [-2, 0]$ and the condition
  becomes $|1 - 2\lambda| \le 1$, i.e.\ $\lambda \le 1$.  The sigmoid
  parameterisation $\lambda = \sigma(\beta) \in (0,1)$ certifies this
  by construction, and the linearised $\ell_2$ norm is
  non--increasing:
  $\|p^{k+1}\|_2 \le \|I + \lambda J_D\|_2\, \|p^k\|_2 \le \|p^k\|_2$.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:pde}]
  The DTFN update with shared parameters $\theta$ and per--step CFL
  $\lambda = T/K$ is
  \[
    p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \tfrac{T}{K}\bigl( F(p^k_{i-1}, p^k_i; \theta)
                - F(p^k_i, p^k_{i+1}; \theta) \bigr),
  \]
  which is exactly the first--order conservative finite--difference
  discretisation of $\partial_t p + \partial_x F(p;\theta) = 0$ on a
  uniform grid with mesh width $h$ in $x$ and time step $\Delta t =
  T/K = \lambda h$ in $t$.  By the Crandall--Majda monotonicity
  theorem \cite{crandall1980monotone}, a monotone, conservative,
  consistent scheme converges to the unique Kru\v{z}kov entropy
  solution of the scalar conservation law as $h, \Delta t \to 0$
  provided the CFL condition $\lambda \rho(J_F) \le 1$ holds.  The
  MonotoneFluxNet is constructed to be monotone
  (Section~\ref{sec:method}), conservative (telescoping fluxes,
  Theorem~\ref{thm:conservation}), and consistent (the
  $v \cdot \text{left}$ term recovers the upwind flux at
  initialisation, and the Softplus MLP perturbs it continuously).  The
  CFL condition is certified by Theorem~\ref{thm:cfl}.  Hence
  $p^k_i \to p(i h, k \Delta t)$ in $L^1$ as $K \to \infty$.

  For the MUSCL variant, the same argument applies with the
  second--order Lax--Wendroff--type convergence rate
  $\mathcal{O}(h^2)$, provided the Van~Leer limiter is used (the
  scheme remains TVD, Theorem~\ref{thm:tvd}) and the CFL condition
  holds \cite{leveque2002finite}.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\begin{proof}[Proof sketch of Theorem~\ref{thm:univ}]
  The proof has three parts.

  \emph{Step 1 (FluxNet universality).}  The MonotoneFluxNet
  $F(\text{left}, \text{right}; \theta)$ is a two--input, single--output
  MLP with Softplus output.  By the Cybenko--Hornik universal
  approximation theorem \cite{hornik1991approximation}, a single
  hidden layer of sufficient width can approximate any continuous
  function on a compact set to arbitrary accuracy.  The monotonicity
  constraints ($|W_\text{left}|$, $-|W_\text{right}|$, $|W_\text{out}|$)
  restrict the hypothesis class to monotone fluxes, but any
  \emph{monotone} continuous flux can be approximated: the monotone
  cone is dense in itself, and the Softplus--output MLP with signed
  weights can realise any non--negative continuous function on a
  compact set (Hornik's theorem applied to the non--negative cone).

  \emph{Step 2 (Stacking realises any local map).}  A single FV
  update couples cell $i$ to $\{i-1, i, i+1\}$; $K$ stacked updates
  couple $i$ to $\{i-K, \dots, i+K\}$, giving a receptive field of
  $2K+1$.  Conversely, any translation--equivariant local map with
  receptive field $r = 2K+1$ can be written as a sequence of $K$
  pairwise couplings (a product of shift--invariant kernels), each
  realisable by a MonotoneFluxNet.  This is the discrete analogue of
  the result that any local causal operator can be decomposed into a
  product of infinitesimal couplings.

  \emph{Step 3 (Decoder universality).}  The per--token decoder
  $\mathrm{dec}: \R^d \to \R^V$ is a 2--layer MLP with GELU, which is
  a universal approximator \cite{hornik1991approximation} on the
  per--token mass.

  Combining the three steps: for any continuous local
  translation--equivariant $\mathcal{F}$ and any $\varepsilon > 0$,
  choose $K$ so that the receptive field $2K+1 \ge r$, choose the
  FluxNet width to approximate the per--coupling flux to
  $\varepsilon/3$, and choose the decoder width to approximate the
  per--token readout to $\varepsilon/3$.  The triangle inequality
  gives the final $\varepsilon$ bound.
  \qed
\end{proof}

\subsection{Proof of Theorem~\ref{thm:fct} (FCT conservation and positivity)}
\label{app:fct-proof}

\begin{proof}
\textbf{(a) Conservation.}
The FCT update is $p^{k+1}_i = p^k_i + \lambda (F^{\mathrm{FCT}}_{i-1/2} -
F^{\mathrm{FCT}}_{i+1/2})$, where $F^{\mathrm{FCT}} = F_{\mathrm{low}} +
\phi \odot A$ and $A = F_{\mathrm{high}} - F_{\mathrm{low}}$.
Both $F_{\mathrm{low}}$ and $A$ are fluxes (they live on face
indices), so summing the update over all cells:
\begin{align*}
  \sum_i p^{k+1}_i
  &= \sum_i p^k_i + \lambda \sum_i (F^{\mathrm{FCT}}_{i-1/2} - F^{\mathrm{FCT}}_{i+1/2}) \\
  &= \sum_i p^k_i + \lambda \Bigl[
       \sum_i F_{\mathrm{low},i-1/2} - \sum_i F_{\mathrm{low},i+1/2}
       + \sum_i (\phi_{i-1/2} A_{i-1/2}) - \sum_i (\phi_{i+1/2} A_{i+1/2})
     \Bigr].
\end{align*}
Each bracketed sum telescopes to zero (with zero--flux boundary
conditions $F_{-1/2} = F_{L-1/2} = 0$), so $\sum_i p^{k+1}_i = \sum_i
p^k_i$.

\textbf{(b) Positivity.}
Let $p_{\mathrm{low}} = p + \lambda (F_{\mathrm{low}}^{\mathrm{in}} -
F_{\mathrm{low}}^{\mathrm{out}})$ be the low--order update, with
$p_{\mathrm{low}} \ge 0$ by the monotonicity of $F_{\mathrm{low}}$ and
the standard DTFN positivity step.  The FCT update can be written as
$p^{k+1} = p_{\mathrm{low}} + \lambda \phi \odot (A^{\mathrm{in}} -
A^{\mathrm{out}})$, where $A^{\mathrm{in}}_i = A_{i-1/2}$ and
$A^{\mathrm{out}}_i = A_{i+1/2}$.  Let $\Delta A_i = A^{\mathrm{in}}_i
- A^{\mathrm{out}}_i$ denote the net anti--diffusive contribution to
cell $i$.  Define the Zalesak coefficient
\[
  \alpha_i = \min\!\left(1,\;
    \frac{p_{\max,i} - p_{\mathrm{low},i}}{\max(\Delta A_i, 0) + \varepsilon},\;
    \frac{p_{\mathrm{low},i} - p_{\min,i}}{\max(-\Delta A_i, 0) + \varepsilon}
  \right),
\]
where $p_{\max,i}, p_{\min,i}$ are the local extrema of $p_{\mathrm{low}}$
in the stencil of cell $i$.  If $\phi_{i\pm 1/2} \le \min(\alpha_i,
\alpha_{i+1})$ for every face adjacent to cell $i$, then
$p_{\mathrm{low},i} + \lambda \phi \Delta A_i \in [p_{\min,i},
p_{\max,i}] \subseteq [0, \infty)$, so $p^{k+1}_i \ge 0$.  Our
implementation enforces this by computing $\alpha_i$ for each cell
and taking $\phi_{i+1/2} \leftarrow \min(\phi_{i+1/2}, \alpha_i,
\alpha_{i+1})$ before the update.

\textbf{(c) High--order recovery.}
In smooth regions, the Jameson smoothness sensor $\sigma_i \to 0$ (it
is $\mathcal{O}(\Delta x^2)$ for smooth $p$), so the analytic limiter
$\phi = 1 - \sigma \to 1$ and $F^{\mathrm{FCT}} = F_{\mathrm{low}} + A
= F_{\mathrm{high}}$, recovering the high--order flux.  For the
learned limiter, if $\mathrm{LimiterNet}_\psi$ outputs $\phi \to 1$ on
smooth inputs (which is the supervised target), the same recovery
holds.
\end{proof}

\section{Implementation details}
\label{app:implementation}

Our PyTorch implementation comprises approximately 300 lines of code
in \texttt{dtfn\_core.py} and 350 lines of experiment scripts in
\texttt{dtfn\_experiments\_v2.py}.  Key hyperparameters:

\begin{itemize}[leftmargin=*,itemsep=2pt]
  \item Embedding dimension: $d=64$.
  \item Number of update steps: $K=3$ (DTFN--U/B/MUSCL), $K=4$ (DTFN--FULL).
  \item FluxNet hidden width: $128$.
  \item Initial CFL logit: $0$ (so $\lambda = 0.5$ at initialisation).
  \item Positivity floor: $\Eps = 10^{-6}$.
  \item Optimiser: Adam, learning rate $10^{-3}$, batch size $32$.
  \item Gradient clipping: $\|\nabla\|_2 \le 1$.
  \item Epochs: $5$ (Dyck--1), $8$ (Dyck--2), $5$ (extrap, copy).
\end{itemize}

All experiments were run on an Apple M4 CPU with $16$~GB RAM.
Training the full Dyck--1 multi--seed comparison (6 models $\times$ 5
seeds $\times$ 5 epochs) takes approximately $25$ minutes.  The
scaling experiments (Figure~\ref{fig:scaling}) use a batch size of
$8$ and average over $5$ forward passes after $2$ warmup iterations.
